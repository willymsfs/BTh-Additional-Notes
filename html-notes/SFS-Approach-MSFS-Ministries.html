Preliminary note on apostolates of MSFS

The foundational apostolates of the MSFS Congregation were parish mission preaching, education of the young, and missions abroad. These apostolates developed over time as the Congregation responded to the needs and challenges of the Church and transient society.

Parish mission preaching (Art 24-30), also known as preaching missions/renewal of Christian life, was a key apostolate of the MSFS Congregation since its foundation. Fr. Mermier believed that preaching missions was an effective way to renew the faith of Catholics and bring back those who had strayed from the Church. As such, the MSFS dedicated themselves to preaching missions in various parishes throughout France and beyond.

Education and Formation of the young (Art 36-43) was another important apostolate for the MSFS Congregation. Fr. Mermier believed that providing a solid Christian education was essential for the formation of missionary leaders. As a result, the MSFS established schools and colleges where young people could receive a Salesian value-based education on the teachings of St. Francis de Sales.

Missions abroad (Art 31-35) were also an important part of the MSFS apostolate. Fr. Mermier believed that it was important for the Congregation to reach out to areas where the Gospel had not yet been preached. As a result, the MSFS sent missionaries to various parts of the world, including Africa, Asia, and South America, to establish Christian communities and renew the faith of those already living there.

Overall, the MSFS Congregation has continued to adapt and respond to the changing needs of the Church and society, while remaining faithful to its foundational charism and apostolates. (Reading the signs of the times cold be mentioned as alternative)

Five solae of the Protestant Reformation

The Five Solae (or "solas") of the Protestant Reformation were a set of slogans that summarized the main teachings of the Protestant Reformers on the nature of salvation and the authority of the Bible. These solae are:

Sola Scriptura: The authority of the Bible as the sole source of religious authority, rejecting the Catholic Church's role as interpreter of scripture.

Sola Fide: Salvation is a gift of God's grace received through faith in Jesus Christ, not earned through good works or sacraments.

Sola Gratia: Salvation is entirely a work of God's grace, not dependent on individual merit or good works.

Solus Christus: Salvation is obtained through Jesus Christ alone, not through other means such as intercession of saints or observance of rituals.

Soli Deo Gloria: The ultimate purpose of salvation is to bring glory to God, not for personal gain.

The Five Points of Calvinism, also known as the "Doctrines of Grace," are a summary of the main teachings of the theologian John Calvin and his followers on the nature of salvation and the sovereignty of God. These points are:

1. Total depravity: Calvinists believe that all people are born in a state of sin and are unable to do good or please God on their own.

2. Unconditional election: Calvinists believe that God has chosen, before the foundations of the world, those who will be saved, and this choice is based on his sovereign will and not on any merit or good deeds of the individuals in question.

3. Limited atonement: Calvinists believe that the death of Jesus Christ on the cross was intended to save only the elect, and not all people.

4. Irresistible grace: Calvinists believe that when God chooses to save an individual, that person will inevitably come to faith in Jesus Christ and be saved.

5. Perseverance of the saints: Calvinists believe that those who are truly saved will persevere in their faith and cannot lose their salvation.

Education and Formation in the Protestant Reformation and Catholic Counter-Reformation

The Protestant Reformation and Catholic humanism were both movements that placed a significant emphasis on education and instruction. He defended the value and importance of the education and formation of women in his time. Francis de Sales believed that education was a key tool for bringing people close to God. He was particularly interested in women's education and encouraged women to take an active role in the religious life. He believed that education was a means for individuals to develop their full potential and lead a life of holiness.

Skill to Work for Peace and Justice

St. Francis de Sales was indeed deeply engaged in the political, economic, and social issues of his time, and sought to build a more just and peaceful world through his actions as a bishop and spiritual leader. He used his authority and influence as "Prince and Bishop of Geneva" to advocate for social and economic justice, particularly in regards to the fair treatment of priests and the poor. He worked to reform or close monasteries that were engaging in scandalous behaviour and redirecting their income away from its intended purpose of supporting priests and needy parishes in the diocese. He also engaged in political negotiations on behalf of his Prince and his Diocese, striving for peace and justice in the broader community. Through his writings, particularly in his letters, he also advocated for a peaceful and just society. St Francis de Sales exemplified the Christian virtue of justice, in practical way by his actions and words.

He wrote several books on the spiritual life, including the "Introduction to the Devout Life" and "Treatise on the Love of God", which have been translated into many languages and continue to be read and studied today. He also possessed great rhetorical and communication skills, able to reach and convince to people of different social and cultural backgrounds.

Skill of Administration

As the bishop of Geneva, Francis de Sales was committed to spreading the teachings of the Council of Trent and educating his diocese in the doctrines of the Church. He preached frequently, organized diocesan synods, and initiated the practice of parish visitations, visiting the entirety of his diocese twice. He also formed the Confraternity of Christian Doctrine (CCD) and personally taught catechism classes, even inventing a type of sign language to teach a young man born deaf how to pray.

However, there were also administrative challenges during his tenure. He faced issues with the religious life in his diocese, which was in a poor state due to ignorance, immorality, avarice and arrogance among the clergy. Despite resistance, he was able to reform the monastic orders by re-instituting penitential practices. He also established the Florimontane Academy, a school for the arts and sciences, but it existed for only three years and he faced three lawsuits as a result. Despite these challenges, Francis de Sales maintained a direct and personal approach with the people in his care, and he did not let setbacks discourage him from continuing to work for the betterment of his diocese and the people within it.

Above all the Skill of Virtues over Values leading to Holiness

Catechism of the Catholic Church (CCC) number 1803 defines virtue as "A virtue is an habitual and firm disposition to do the good. It allows the person not only to perform good acts, but to give the best of himself. The virtuous person tends toward the good with all his sensory and spiritual powers; he pursues the good and chooses it in concrete actions." So, virtue can be considered a basic life skill we need to reach excellence in a society.

On the other hand, values are individual and subjective. For example, someone may value photography and possess technical knowledge of the field, but this does not necessarily mean they possess the virtue of good photography. Lacking virtue may negatively impact not only oneself but also those around them, such as making rash decisions instead of prudent ones.

Unlike values, virtues possess certain traits such as being habitual, done with ease, promptness and bringing joy. Furthermore, values may lead to competency and effectiveness, but virtues have the ability to make us holy. A person who places a high importance on moral values, may wait for external recognition, while a virtuous person acts out of pleasing God rather than external validation. In today's society, where the use of social media is prominent, values may be used as a means of seeking attention, whereas virtues focus on pleasing God alone.

So, SFS emphasizes the importance of selecting specific virtues to focus on, and how to practice these virtues in a way that is most beneficial to ourselves and others. He also stresses the importance of practicing virtues that align with our duty and calling, rather than simply those that align with our personal tastes or preferences. He also encourages individuals to cultivate the more subtle and internal virtues, such as humility and meekness, rather than focusing solely on external acts of piety. Additionally, he encourages the integration of action with prayer and devotions and advises how to avoid the false humility. He encourages people to take time to reflect on what virtues they need to cultivate, and to focus on one virtue at a time as an object and pursuit of their mind, in a way that does not neglect other virtues. He also stresses the importance of being honest and sincere, and to be transparent in their actions and intentions.

Conclusion:

In conclusion, Francis de Sales' contributions to education and formation continue to be relevant and inspiring, particularly in regards to the importance of education, the value of a holistic approach, and an optimistic attitude on the part of the educator and formator. Despite changes in the world and in the concept of the human being, these points remain fundamental.

St. Francis de Sales viewed education as a holistic process of preparing the full individual - body, soul, and mind - to live a good and holy life in the world. He felt that education should emphasise the individual's development and integration into society, with a particular emphasis on the cultivation of virtues, self-control, and the balance of passions, emotions, and vices.

He viewed the human being as the "perfection of the universe" and stressed the significance of caring for the body, as well as the need to educate the senses and passions, in order to achieve self-control and balance in life. He also emphasised the significance of the intellect, memory, and will, as well as their roles in rational thought, comprehension, and decision-making, for the development of an individual.

He emphasised the importance of intelligence and comprehension and believed that a well-rounded education should involve both secular and spiritual growth. In addition, he emphasises memorising and the training of memory to aid in the preservation of vital information and the practise of virtue.

In his writings, he encourages individuals to labour in society and for society and to be responsible stewards who fight for the rights of society and individuals, expressing their disdain for societal vices. In addition, he envisions an individual who is creative, moral, and capable of focusing on the love of self, neighbour, and God. Overall, St. Francis de Sales' approach to education emphasised personal formation, integration into society, and the cultivation of virtues, which he considered were essential for living a holy and virtuous life.

Education is crucial for the development of individuals and society as a whole. It shapes values, beliefs and attitudes and helps individuals acquire the skills needed to succeed in personal and professional life. Education promotes social cohesion, addresses issues of inequality and injustice, and is vital for the formation of a just and equitable society. Education is necessary to develop life coping skills we all humans need in order to live life in this complex world.
