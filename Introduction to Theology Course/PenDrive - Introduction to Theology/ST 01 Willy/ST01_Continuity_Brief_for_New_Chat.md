## 2. NON-NEGOTIABLE STYLE RULES (apply to every output)

- No em dashes. Use commas, colons, semicolons, or parentheses.
- No contractions. Write "do not", "it is", "cannot", "you are".
- Avoid apostrophes and possessive forms wherever possible. Rephrase: "the will of God", not "God's will"; "the work of the theologian", not "the theologian's work".
- Citations follow Turabian.
- Scripture follows the Revised Standard Version, Catholic Edition (RSV-2CE).
- General explanation follows the style of Gaur Gopal Das: simple language, gentle humour, relatable illustration, practical wisdom, reflective spiritual insight.
- Student notes and class notes are written in flowing prose, not bullet points.
- Forbidden vocabulary, never to be used in prose: Dichotomy, exploration, solely, Underscore, Emphasis, emphasize/emphasise/emphasizing, Tapestry, Leverage, Journey, Resonate, Testament, Explore, Navigate, Binary, perspective, Delve, Enrich, Seamless, Multifaceted, Foster, Convey, Beacon, Interplay, skewed, Adhere, Landscape, Accent, Paramount, Comprehensive, Realm, Summary, framework, Symphony, Furthermore, Relationship, Profound, Ultimately, Art, Evolve, Beyond, Reimagine, Vibrant, Robust, Pivotal, Certainly, Orchestrate, Align, Diverse, Recommend, Annals, Note, Employ, Indeed, Enigma, Indelible, Refrain, Treat, Meticulous, Esteem, Highlight, Commendable, Unlock, Key, Breakdown, Treasure, Especially, Paradigm, Captivate, Delve into, A testament to, In conclusion, A treasure trove of, In this article, A tapestry of, In the annals of, Let us explore, Final thoughts, Rapid, Transformative, Uncharted, Inclusive, Unprecedented, Innovative, Critical.
- Two exceptions on "critical": the fixed technical terms "critical reflection on praxis" (the definition of Gutiérrez), "biblical criticism", and "historical-critical method" are allowed. Otherwise avoid the word; use "searching", "evaluative", "rigorous", "frank".

## 3. THE LOGIC AND THE SETTLED DECISIONS (preserve all of these)

**The pedagogy.** The course is problem-based, not a list of topics. Every lecture opens with a real theological problem so that the student searches rather than stores. The course is one sustained argument with a destination, the sixth goal.

**The six goals (the spine of questions).** By the last day every student can answer: (1) What is theology? (2) Why do theology? (3) How is theology done? (4) Who has authority in theology? (5) How has theology developed through history? (6) How do I become a responsible theologian today? Goal 6 is the destination; it is seeded at Lecture 5 (vocation) and returns in every unit.

**The Lonergan precept spine.** The four precepts of Bernard Lonergan run through the course: be attentive (governs Unit III, the sources), be intelligent (Unit IV, history), be reasonable (Unit V, method), be responsible (Unit VI). "Responsible" in Goal 6 is the fourth precept; the form of the course is also its goal.

**The class rhythm (45 minutes).** Ten minutes opening problem, fifteen minutes teaching, ten minutes discussion through the reading rotation, five minutes reflection, five minutes assignment. The rhythm of the hour repeats the four precepts in miniature.

**The reading rotation.** Each class three students carry the reading: a summariser (gives the argument in two minutes), a critic (names one weakness), a responder (answers or deepens). The professor moderates. This replaces a separate book review.

**The uploaded manual.** A house manual titled "Introduction to Theology" exists and is used critically, as the specimen of the older dogmatic method and as a factual reference, not as the spine. It has anti-Protestant and anti-Pentecostal polemic that contradicts its own sixth mark (ecumenical), and small slips (Arianism printed "Aryanism", Irenaeus "Iranaeus", Peshitta "Pshitha"). Correct such slips silently in student notes.

**Unit V is built on Bevans.** The six models of contextual theology of Stephen Bevans (translation, anthropological, praxis, synthetic, transcendental, countercultural) order Unit V; the streams (liberation, Dalit, Indian, feminist) are slotted into the models. Orthodox theology keeps a dedicated lecture (29).

**Assessment (no sit-down exam).** Journal 20%, cumulative glossary 10%, parish-priest interview 10%, theological autobiography 15%, mini research paper 20%, Theology Manifesto (written plus oral, the capstone) 15%, participation 10%. The Manifesto replaces the exam; its question round safeguards the vocabulary. Sized to roughly two to two and a half ECTS; to reach exactly two ECTS, drop either the autobiography or the interview and add its weight to the journal. Library treasure-hunt missions feed participation. Daily reading capped at eight to twelve pages.

**The contextual grounding.** India is the *locus theologicus*. The method is experience, reflection, commitment, joined to See, Judge, Act, and to the contextual biblical method of George Soares-Prabhu. The cry of the poor, the Dalit, the Adivasi, the woman, and the wounded earth is real theological data.

## 4. THE FINALIZED 35-LECTURE BACKBONE (with readings)

**UNIT I — Discovering Theology (Goal 2; seeds Goal 6)**
1. Can a priest preach without doing theology? — Manual pp.1-2.
2. If I already believe, why must I understand? — Anselm Proslogion proem; Manual p.1; Ormerod Ch.1 pp.3-9.
3. How does God get through to us? (Revelation) — Dei Verbum ch.1 §§2-6; Manual pp.11-12.
4. Is faith a leap in the dark or a way of seeing? (Faith) — Fides et Ratio §§1-15.
5. Is theology a job or a calling? (Vocation; seeds Goal 6) — Donum Veritatis §§6-12; Orientation handout. (Assign Unit I paper, 500 words, "Why do I want to study theology?")

**UNIT II — The Nature of Theology (Goal 1)**
6. Why did Jesus never write a theology book? — Ormerod Ch.1 pp.10-14; Müller.
7. Has theology always meant the same thing? (Part one: biblical, patristic, scholastic) — Manual pp.1-2, 5-7; Müller.
8. Has theology always meant the same thing? (Part two: modern, contemporary; the working definition) — Ormerod Ch.3 pp.27-37.
9. Is theology a science? (debate) — Ormerod Ch.4 pp.42-52.
10. What makes theology true, and not heresy? (the marks) — Manual p.2, p.4, p.6.

**UNIT III — The Sources of Theology (Goal 4; be attentive)**
11. Why are there hundreds of theologians if truth is one? — Ormerod Ch.2 pp.15-26.
12. Is the Bible enough? (Scripture) — Dei Verbum ch.3 §§11-13; Manual pp.9-11.
13. Can the dead vote? (Tradition) — Dei Verbum ch.2 §§7-10; Manual pp.3-4.
14. Who decides when we disagree? (Magisterium and sensus fidelium) — Lumen Gentium §12; Donum Veritatis §§13-21.
15. Can reason and experience be sources too? — Manual pp.3-4; Gutiérrez, A Theology of Liberation, ch.1.
16. How did Vatican II re-map the sources? (Dei Verbum) — Dei Verbum ch.6 §§21-26; Ormerod Ch.4 pp.52-57.

**UNIT IV — The History of Theology (Goal 5; be intelligent; teach the question each age asked)**
17. Why does every age produce a different theology? — Manual pp.5-8.
18. Who is this Jesus? (Patristic) — Manual pp.5-7; Nicene Creed with the Arian controversy.
19. Can faith and reason live together? (Scholastic) — Manual p.7; Fides et Ratio §§43-48.
20. Who saves, and how? (Reformation and Trent) — Manual p.8; a short reading on justification.
21. How do we believe in a doubting world? (Modern) — Manual pp.8-9; Gaudium et Spes §§1-4.
22. What did the Church wake up to? (Vatican II) — Manual pp.8-9; Gaudium et Spes §§4-10.

**UNIT V — The Methods of Theology (Goal 3; be reasonable; the heart; Bevans)**
23. Is theology information or method? — Ormerod Ch.4 pp.42-57; Segundo on the hermeneutic circle.
24. Three ways into one mystery (the branches) — Manual pp.4-5; Müller on the disciplines.
25. Models, not chaos (Bevans, six models) — Bevans, Models of Contextual Theology (the notes carry a full overview).
26. Can the poor produce theology? (liberation and Dalit; praxis model) — Nirmal, the founding Dalit essay; recap of the praxis model.
27. Theology in the mother tongue (Indian, inculturation; anthropological and synthetic models) — Amaladoss in Theologizing in India; recap of the models.
28. Whose voice was missing? (feminist; countercultural) — Ormerod, Johnson portrait, pp.185-194.
29. Theology on the knees (Orthodox; worship and mystery) — Ormerod, Meyendorff portrait, pp.205-214; Evagrius, On Prayer, opening.

**UNIT VI — Doing Theology Today (Goal 6; be responsible; the destination)**
30. Can AI do theology? — Antiqua et Nova (2025), selected sections.
31. Can the Church think together? (synodality) — a short reading from the 2024 Synod Final Document.
32. Is the earth a theological text? (ecology) — Laudato Si' §§1-16.
33. How do faiths theologize together? (dialogue, pluralism) — Amaladoss on dialogue, Theologizing in India.
34. What makes a theologian responsible today? — Donum Veritatis §§24-41; recap of the four precepts.
35. My Theology Manifesto (capstone, oral plus written, and a blessing) — no reading; review journal and glossary; write the manifesto.

## 7. THE TEACHING-NOTES FORMAT TEMPLATE (reproduce exactly)

Each teaching-notes part opens with a short prose section, "The Arc of the Unit", naming the goal the unit serves, the precept it carries, and the move of each lecture. Then each lecture follows this structure, in flowing prose with light labelled lead-ins:

- **Title line:** "LECTURE N: [the driving question]".
- **The opening problem.** How to pose the day, the tension to hold, the turn to the answer.
- **The teaching content.** Fuller than the professor will speak; accurate; the concepts, distinctions, and scriptural and doctrinal anchors.
- **Illustration.** One or two fresh, warm, teachable images for the board (Gaur Gopal Das style). Never repeat an illustration already used (see section 8).
- **References.** Real, Turabian-compatible: author, work, and locus. Scripture in RSV-2CE.
- **Cross-references.** Where the idea returns across the thirty-five lectures and the six goals.
- **For the professor.** A teaching tip, plus the reading and the journal reflection prompt for that lecture.

Each part closes with a short prose section, "Closing the Unit, and the Bridge to the next", and ends with the standard signature block: "Prepared by Rev. Prof. William Anthony, MSFS / Morning Star Regional Seminary, Barrackpore, June 2026."

Depth target: each lecture entry is several solid paragraphs of teaching content, not a skeleton. The professor wants to be made thorough.
