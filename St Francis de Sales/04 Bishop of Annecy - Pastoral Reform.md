# 04 — Bishop of Annecy: Pastoral Reform (1602–1622)

> [!abstract] Thesis
> His twenty years as bishop are **Trent enacted at ground level by a man who refuses to enforce it harshly**. The programme is severe; the manner is gentle. That combination is the Salesian pastoral signature.

---

## 1. The seven works of the reforming bishop

| Field | What he did |
|---|---|
| **Catechesis** | Founded Sunday-schools in Annecy, then across the diocese; taught the children himself, with evident joy |
| **Preaching** | Turned the pulpit away from controversy toward morals and instruction; short, plain, heartfelt |
| **Visitation** | Systematically visited all **435 parishes**, crossing ice-covered mountain passes to reach remote flocks |
| **Clergy formation** | Rigorous examination before appointment; a **manual for confessors**; regular **synods**; repeated petitions to Rome for a diocesan **seminary** |
| **Religious houses** | Restored observance at the Augustinian Abbey of **Sixt** and the Bernardines of **St Catherine's** — by patience and persuasion against fierce resistance |
| **The poor** | Pawned, repeatedly, the diamond ring given him by the Princess of Savoy to feed the starving during famine; locals joked the ring belonged to the town's beggars |
| **Administration** | Simplicity of household, refusal of a carriage, strict frugality for his staff |

## 2. Preaching reform — the test of a sermon

> [!quote] Camus records the criterion
> *"Do you wish to know how I test the excellence and value of a preacher? It is by assuring myself that those who have been listening to him come away striking their breasts and saying: 'I will do better'; not by their saying: 'Oh how well he spoke, what beautiful things he said!'"*

And on style: *"Our words must be enkindled, not by exaggerated shouting or elaborate gestures, but by our inner love. Words must come from the heart rather than the mouth, for the heart speaks to the heart while the tongue speaks only to the ear."*

He held that *"multiplicity of words does not produce great results"* and that *"there is no more disagreeable quality in a preacher than prolixity."*

> [!example] The rebuke of Camus
> When Jean-Pierre Camus preached a flowery panegyric of Francis in his own cathedral, the saint rebuked him severely: *"What could have induced you to play these pranks? Do you know that you spoilt your sermon by them? Truly, I am a fine sort of salt, fit only to be thrown into the street and trampled under foot."*

## 3. Household and daily life

Public dignity of a prince-bishop; private life of extreme frugality.

- Rented house in Annecy. Handsome public rooms; for himself a **small, dimly lit room** — bed, table, chairs, writing desk.
- Staff required to wear plain, cheap clothing. **No carriage.**
- Rose early; **an hour of mental prayer**; Mass at 9 a.m.; dinner at 10 a.m., supper at 6 p.m., with holy reading during the first part of meals.
- Fasted Fridays and Saturdays; evenings for reading and study.
- **Endlessly available** to any soul seeking counsel — the non-negotiable of his day.

## 4. The letters — over 2,000

A genre in itself: practical, affectionate, and **customised**. Written for laypeople in every walk of life — widows like Chantal, court ladies like Madame de Charmoisy. No monastic austerities imposed on people living in the world; instead rules of conduct, humility, patience, and the **faithful execution of daily duties**.

> [!important] The letters are the laboratory
> The *Introduction to the Devout Life* **is** a set of these letters, edited. The doctrine of the universal call to holiness was not deduced from a principle; it was **extracted from twenty years of correspondence with laypeople who were actually becoming holy where they were**.

---

## 5. Evaluation — Salesian pastoral style in five words

1. **Presence** — 435 parishes, on foot, over ice.
2. **Instruction** — catechism for children, examinations for clergy.
3. **Simplicity** — a bishop with no carriage and a borrowed room.
4. **Patience** — monastic reform by persuasion, not decree.
5. **Availability** — the door open, always, to any soul.

> [!tip] For homiletic or formation use
> The pawned diamond ring is the single best image of Salesian episcopacy: **the insignia of rank liquidated into bread**, again and again, until the poor effectively own it.
