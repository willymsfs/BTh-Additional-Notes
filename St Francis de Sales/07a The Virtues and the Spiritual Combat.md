# 07a — The Virtues and the Spiritual Combat

> [!abstract] Thesis
> Salesian asceticism is **governed by vocation, not by taste**. You do not choose the virtues you admire; you practise the ones your state of life actually demands — and you fight temptation by turning away, not by wrestling.

---

## 1. Choosing your virtues

**Rule 1 — vocation, not attraction.** Choose the virtues *"most conformable to our duties"*, not those we find appealing. Bishop, prince, soldier, married woman, widow: all called to devotion, each with different requisite virtues.

**Rule 2 — excellence, not display.** Prefer the most **excellent** virtues to the most **esteemed**. The world values severe bodily austerity; gentleness, modesty and **mortification of the heart** are far superior.

**Rule 3 — the ordinary ones are the frequent ones.** The virtues most often required in daily life are the "lesser" general ones: **gentleness, temperance, modesty, humility**.

> [!important] Charity is the queen
> Charity is *"the queen of virtues"* and *"the sun among the stars"*, giving life, beauty and perfection to all the rest. Without it the other virtues are dead or badly imperfect. **Devotion is charity's flame**: if charity is spiritual fire, devotion is the blaze that makes us prompt, active and diligent → [[05 Introduction to the Devout Life]].

## 2. The virtues in detail

### Patience
Necessary *"to possess our souls."* Extends beyond honourable tribulation (persecution for religion) to the **ignominious and abject**: contempt from friends, illness at an inconvenient time. The patient person **does not complain or fish for pity**, but states the ill truthfully and simply.

### Obedience
- **Necessary obedience**: ecclesiastical, civil, and domestic superiors (parents, masters).
- **Perfect obedience**: cheerfully following superiors' *counsels* and *inclinations*; obeying in disagreeable matters; **yielding even to equals and inferiors** where reason permits.

> [!tip] The sharp test
> Obedience to a **harsh** superior proves more than obedience to a gentle one — it shows you are obeying **God**, not your affection for the person.

### Chastity and purity of heart
*"The lily of the virtues"*, making us almost equal to angels. Requires absolute refusal of forbidden gratification and moderation of even lawful pleasures in marriage. Be **prompt to turn away** — the evil works insensibly.

**Sovereign remedy:** closeness to **Jesus Christ crucified**, through meditation and Holy Communion.

> [!note] Purity defined negatively — and mercifully
> Purity of heart does **not** mean freedom from temptation or sinful desire, *which is impossible*. It means **never consenting** to them.

### Poverty
| Condition | Practice |
|---|---|
| Rich in fact | Be **poor in spirit**: keep the heart empty of inordinate attachment |
| Rich, and serious about it | Actually **deprive yourself** — give generously, associate with the poor, treat them as equals |
| Losing wealth (disaster, lawsuit) | Embrace it peacefully and joyfully — proof the goods were held by your *care*, not your *heart* |
| Poor by circumstance | Make a virtue of necessity, without complaint. Poverty **despised and unassisted** is the most excellent kind |

### Humility — exterior and interior
- **Exterior:** avoid vain display, but do **not stubbornly refuse honours** required by duty or civility.
- **Interior:** profound recognition of one's nothingness; every good attributed to grace.
- **Against false humility:** self-deprecating words one does not believe, uttered to win praise, are *"the quintessence of the most subtle pride."*
- **Summit:** **love your abjection** — joy at being despised, corrected, thought foolish.

### Reputation
> [!warning] The balance most people get wrong
> Loving abjection does **not** mean neglecting your good name. Humility and charity **demand** care for reputation, because it is necessary for human society and the **edification of the neighbour**. But: never be touchy or anxious about it.

Under calumny: (1) state your innocence calmly and truthfully; (2) if the slander persists, **conquer it with silence, patience and constancy**, leaving your name in God's hands.

### Meekness toward oneself
Do not fret, rage, or be bitterly indignant at your own faults. Correct yourself with **tranquil, dispassionate, compassionate repentance**, raising the heart gently back to God → [[07 Salesian Themes - Devout Humanism]] Theme 4.

## 3. Holy zeal vs bitter zeal

| **Holy zeal** | **Bitter zeal** |
|---|---|
| The ardour of charity | Passion disguised as righteousness |
| Patient, kind, diligent, careful | Impetuous, fierce, harsh |
| Its highest use: **suffering and enduring much to hinder evil** | Pardons nothing; exaggerates the smallest faults |
| Corrects with sweetness | Acts from anger and pride |

*"More flies are caught with a spoonful of honey than with a hundred barrels of vinegar."*

## 4. The spiritual combat

> [!important] The foundational distinction
> **Feeling a temptation ≠ consenting to it.** *As long as the temptation displeases us, we have not consented, and there is no sin.* God permits temptation to test fidelity and increase the crown.

### Great temptations — rare, violent
1. Cry out to God **like a child running to its mother**.
2. Embrace the Cross.
3. Divert the mind with praiseworthy occupation.
4. **Sovereign remedy: reveal it to your spiritual director.**
5. **Never dispute or argue with the enemy.**

### Small daily temptations — the buzzing flies
Irritability, suspicion, jealousy, vanity. Constant, and therefore **the greatest source of merit** because conquering them is constant.

**Do not fight them directly.** Gently drive them away by an **act of the contrary virtue**, or simply turn the heart to **Jesus Christ crucified** in an act of love.

> [!tip] Why the asymmetry
> Disputing with a small temptation gives it your attention, which is exactly what it wants. Salesian combat is **redirection**, not wrestling. This is the ascetical form of the whole method: you do not conquer by force, you conquer by turning toward what is better.

## 5. Sadness, joy, anxiety

| | Diagnosis | Remedy |
|---|---|---|
| **Holy sadness** | Repentance for sin; compassion for others | Legitimate — keep it |
| **Evil sadness / melancholy** | Oppresses the brain, breeds inordinate fears, causes distaste for prayer, paralyses the soul | Persistent prayer; **singing spiritual canticles**; varied exterior work; **acts of fervour performed even when totally dry** |
| **Holy joy** | Devotion is the sweetest of all things | Cultivate moderate, unaffected **gaiety** in company |
| **Anxiety / disquietude** | An eager, immoderate desire to escape an evil or obtain a good | Manage affairs diligently but **without eagerness or solicitude** |

> [!quote] The great maxim on anxiety
> ***"Disquietude is the greatest evil that can befall the soul, except sin."***
> It disturbs reason, spoils the work in hand, and leaves us open to the devil.

## 6. Discernment of inspirations

Inspiration mirrors temptation in reverse: **God proposes → we take delectation → we consent and act.**

> [!important] The three marks of a genuine inspiration
> 1. **Perseverance** — as against inconstancy and fickle levity.
> 2. **Peace and gentleness of heart** — as against disquiet and turbulence.
> 3. **Humble obedience** to the Church and to superiors — the guard against obstinacy and illusion.

**And the anti-scruple rule:** in small everyday matters where two good options barely differ, **do not torture yourself discerning** — choose freely and walk in good faith.

## 7. Perseverance and renewal

Human nature is frail; resolutions weaken. Hence the **annual retreat** (→ [[05a Sacramental and Devotional Life]]):
- review your state toward **God, yourself, your neighbour**;
- **tune the affections** — love, desire, fear, hope, sadness, joy;
- consider the soul's eternal excellence and the beauty of the virtues;
- end in thanksgiving and a **firm renewed protestation** to persevere.

## Recall

- Q:: On what basis do we choose which virtues to practise? A:: Our duties and state of life — not personal attraction
- Q:: What is the "queen of virtues" and the "sun among the stars"? A:: Charity
- Q:: Relation of devotion to charity? A:: Charity is the fire, devotion the flame — charity made prompt and diligent
- Q:: Why is obedience to a harsh superior a better test? A:: It shows we obey God, not our affection for the person
- Q:: Define purity of heart. A:: Not freedom from temptation (impossible), but never consenting to it
- Q:: Which kind of poverty does he call the most excellent? A:: Poverty that is despised and unassisted
- Q:: Does humility permit neglect of one's reputation? A:: No — charity requires it for the neighbour's edification; but without anxiety
- Q:: Distinguish holy zeal and bitter zeal. A:: Holy zeal is charity's ardour, patient and corrective in sweetness; bitter zeal is harsh passion disguised as righteousness
- Q:: Rule for great temptations? A:: Cry to God, embrace the Cross, divert the mind, tell the director — never dispute with the enemy
- Q:: Rule for small daily temptations? A:: Do not fight directly; make an act of the contrary virtue or turn to Christ crucified
- Q:: Quote the maxim on anxiety. A:: "Disquietude is the greatest evil that can befall the soul, except sin"
- Q:: Three marks of a genuine inspiration? A:: Perseverance; peace and gentleness of heart; humble obedience
