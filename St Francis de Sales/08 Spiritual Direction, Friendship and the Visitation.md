# 08 — Spiritual Direction, Friendship and the Visitation

> [!abstract] Thesis
> His doctrine of the soul is inseparable from his **practice with souls**. Direction, friendship and the Visitation are three forms of one conviction: that grace works through **affection freely given**, never through domination.

---

## 1. The principles of Salesian direction

| Principle | Text |
|---|---|
| Freedom is the medium | *"All by love, nothing by constraint."* |
| No spiritual tyranny | *"In the royal galley of divine love there is no galley-slave; all the oarsmen are volunteers."* |
| The burden is the grace | The weight of weak souls is like the plumage of birds: *"heavy, and yet without this load they could neither raise themselves from the ground nor hover in the air."* |
| Detachment as a rule of life | *"Ask for nothing, refuse nothing, imitate Jesus in His cradle."* |
| Adapt to the person | The letters: never monastic austerity imposed on people living in the world |

## 2. Patience with sinners; the optimism about human misery

He treated sinners *"as did the good shepherd of the Gospel, Who left the ninety-nine sheep in the desert to seek after the hundredth"*, and refused to despair of anyone: *"The greatest sinners often become the greatest penitents."*

> [!quote] The theology under the patience
> *"As Job's patience was enthroned on a dung-hill, so God's mercy is raised upon the wretchedness of man; take away man's misery, and what becomes of God's mercy?"*

When a lady fell into a scandalous fault through weakness, he refused to join the condemnation: *"Human misery! human misery!… Oh! happy fault… Her loss will be her gain, and the gain of many others."*

## 3. Humility, praise and calumny

- On false modesty: expressions of humility not from the heart are *"the flower, the cream, and the quintessence of the most subtle pride."* *"The truly humble of heart do not wish to appear humble, but to be humble."*
- On praise: he feared it, saying his admirers were *"sowing the seed of a bitter fruit for me to gather"* — they would assume he went straight to heaven and forget to pray for him.
- On slander: *"Do they say no more than that? Certainly, they cannot know all, they flatter me, they spare me."* He welcomed critics as **allies against his own self-love**.

## 4. Self-love — the master diagnosis

Self-love disguises itself as **zeal** and as **righteous indignation**. *"Whenever we complain, a certain amount of self-love is always at the bottom of the complaint."*

Remedy for a fall through passion or self-love — note the *tone*, which is the whole point:

> [!quote]
> *"Let us as soon as possible prostrate ourselves in spirit before God, saying, with confidence and humility: Have mercy on me, for I am weak. Let us rise again with peace and tranquillity."*

## 5. Spiritual friendship

**The doctrine** (*Introduction* III): general charity for all; **friendship only with those who can share virtue**.

> [!quote]
> *"O Philothea, love everyone with a great love of charity, but have no friendship save with those who can communicate with you in virtuous things; and the more exquisite the virtues are… the more perfect will your friendship be."*

Friends who share devotion and affection *"become one in spirit"* and *"encourage one another, help one another, and lead one another on to good."* Correspondingly fierce warnings against frivolous flirtations and corrupting friendships.

## 6. St Jane Frances de Chantal

Met at **Dijon, Lent 1604**; recognised from a prior vision. The relationship is the lived instance of the doctrine.

> [!quote] His own account of the bond
> *"It seems to me that God has given me to you. I feel more certain of it every day."*

He made a solemn vow to *"guide, assist, serve, and advance his dear daughter… looking upon her soul as his own."*

> [!important] Theological weight
> This friendship is a **locus theologicus**, not biography-colour: it demonstrates that in the Salesian scheme, affection between persons is a **means of grace** rather than a danger to be managed. Freedom, chastity and deep human love are held together, not traded off.

## 7. The Visitation of Holy Mary (1610)

**Purpose.** Founded to receive *"virgins, widows, and matrons who are weak and ill or of advanced years"* — those every other order would refuse.

Francis called himself *"a partisan of the infirm."*

**Design.**
- A hidden, humble life *"without corporal austerities."*
- Originally the sisters could **go out** to visit the sick and destitute (hence "Visitation"); strict enclosure was imposed later.
- The substitution that defines the charism: *"The mortification of the soul was substituted for that of the flesh, the crucifixion of the will for that of the body."*

> [!quote] The spirit of the order
> *"The Spirit of the Visitation is to be one of humble love of God, and extreme sweetness towards our neighbour."*

> [!tip] What the Visitation proves
> It is Salesian anthropology in institutional form. If sanctity were athletic, the weak and elderly would be excluded. Because sanctity is **the crucifixion of the will in love**, the frail are not second-class — they may be better placed.
