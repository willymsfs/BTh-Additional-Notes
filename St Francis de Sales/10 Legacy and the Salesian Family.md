# 10 — Legacy and the Salesian Family

---

## 1. St Vincent de Paul

Mutual reverence, each considering the other the best man he knew.

- **Vincent on Francis:** the best Christian he had ever met; so gentle and dignified *"it seemed as though Christ Himself once more walked the earth."*
- **Francis on Vincent:** *"the holy priest, the best and noblest man"* he had ever known.

> [!note] Significance
> The link matters historically: Vincentian charity to the poor and Salesian gentleness are the two great currents of the French school's *pastoral* side, over against its more austere Bérullian and later Jansenist strands.

## 2. The congregations bearing his name

| Congregation | Founded | By | Purpose |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Order of the Visitation of Holy Mary** | 1610 | Francis de Sales & Jane de Chantal | Hidden, gentle religious life open to the weak and infirm |
| **Missionaries of St Francis de Sales (Fransalians, MSFS)** | **1838** | **Fr Peter Mermier** | Missionary priests — parish missions, preaching, and the foreign missions (India from 1845) |
| **Salesians of Don Bosco (SDB)** | 1859 (Turin) | **St John Bosco** | Youth, with Salesian gentleness and hope as the explicit inspiration |

> [!warning] Correction to the notebook
> The notebook sources give **1834** for the MSFS foundation. **That is wrong.** The correct date is **1838**, at Annecy, under **Bishop Pierre-Joseph Rey** — Mermier's preaching-mission work had begun earlier (from 1830), which is probably the source of the confusion. Use **1838**.

> [!tip] For MSFS use
> Don Bosco took the *name* and the *manner* (gentleness, hope, kindness with the young). Mermier took the *mission method* — the Chablais model of preaching missions to re-evangelise a de-Christianised countryside. Both are legitimate heirs of different halves of the same life: **the Chablais missioner and the gentle director**.

## 3. The universal call to holiness

His central thesis — devotion in every state of life — is the sixteenth-century anticipation of what becomes the modern theology of the laity.

### 3a. Vatican II — *Lumen Gentium* ch. V

**LG 39–42** (*"The Universal Call to Holiness in the Church"*, 21 Nov 1964) teaches that *"all the faithful of Christ of whatever rank or status are called to the fullness of the Christian life and to the perfection of charity"* (LG 40), and that this one holiness is *"cultivated in a multiplicity of ways by the individuals who… advance in the way of a living faith"* (LG 41), each in his own state, condition and duties.

Set that beside the *Introduction*: devotion *"each one according to his kind and vocation"*, and the refusal to *"banish the devout life from the army, from the workshop, from the courts of princes, from the households of married folk."* The 1609 thesis and the 1964 conciliar text state the same doctrine — Francis de Sales three and a half centuries early, and without a theology of the laity to lean on.

> [!info] Citation discipline
> This connection is **not in the notebook sources** — they contain no reference to Vatican II. Cite **LG 39–42 directly from the conciliar text**, not from this unit, and present the link as a theological judgement (which it is), not as something the biographers say.

### 3b. St Thérèse of Lisieux

The Salesian ancestry of the *"little way"* is a commonplace of the literature: the little virtues that grow *"like wild thyme at the foot of the Tree of Life"*, the primacy of small acts done with great love, confidence over fear, gentleness with one's own weakness. The Visitation link is also concrete — Thérèse's sister **Léonie Martin** became a Visitandine at Caen, and Thérèse's own maternal formation ran through Visitation channels.

> [!info] Citation discipline
> Again **not in the notebook** — Thérèse is named there only in passing, with nothing on influence. If you argue the dependence, source it from a Carmelite or Salesian study (or from *Story of a Soul* directly), not from this unit.

## 4. Titles and patronages

|---|---|

| **Doctor of Charity** | Traditional epithet, from the same |
| **Patron of Catholic writers and journalists** (1923, Pius XI) | The Chablais leaflets — evangelisation through the printed word |

## 5. Why he still functions

1. **He solved a problem we still have**: how to speak truth to people who do not accept your authority. His answer — affection precedes conviction; coercion destroys belief — is directly usable.
2. **He democratised holiness** before anyone had a theology of the laity to do it with.
3. **He is the antidote to rigorism** in every century, including the current one: gentleness with oneself as the precondition of perseverance.
4. **He makes mysticism practical**: the ecstasy of act and life relocates the summit from altered consciousness into ordinary charity.
