# 11 — Quotation Bank and Glossary

> [!info] Citation note
> Abbreviations: **IDL** = *Introduction to the Devout Life*; **TLG** = *Treatise on the Love of God*; **CC** = *The Catholic Controversy*; **Camus** = Jean-Pierre Camus, *The Spirit of St Francis de Sales*; **Beahn** = *A Man of Good Zeal*. Quotations are as they stand in the notebook's editions; check a critical edition before publishing.

## A. Quotation bank by theme

### 1. The nature of devotion
> *"In short, devotion is no other thing than a spiritual nimbleness and vivacity, by means of which charity works in us, or we by her, readily and heartily; and as it is the office of charity to make us observe all the commandments of God generally and universally, so it is the office of devotion to make us observe them readily and diligently."* — **IDL**

> *"In the creation God commanded the plants to bring forth their fruit, each after its kind: even so he commands Christians, who are the living plants of his Church, to bring forth fruits of devotion, each one according to his kind and vocation."* — **IDL**

> *"It is an error, nay rather a heresy, to wish to banish the devout life from the army, from the workshop, from the courts of princes, from the households of married folk."*

### 2. Gentleness and meekness
> *"Always be as gentle as you can, and remember that more flies are caught with a spoonful of honey than with a hundred barrels of vinegar. If we must err in one direction or the other, let it be in that of gentleness. No sauce was ever spoilt by too much sugar. The human mind is so constituted that it rebels against harshness, but becomes perfectly tractable under gentle treatment."* — **Camus**

> *"Humility perfects us in regard to God, and gentleness in regard to our neighbour."* — **IDL**

> *"All by love, nothing by constraint."* — **Camus**

> *"In the royal galley of divine love there is no galley-slave; all the oarsmen are volunteers."* — **Camus**

### 3. Humility and self-knowledge
> *"Now the highest point of this humility is not only voluntarily to acknowledge our abjection, but to love it and take pleasure in it, not from any lack of courage and generosity, but to exalt so much the more the divine Majesty and to esteem our neighbour the more in comparison with ourselves."* — **IDL**

> *"To receive the grace of God into our hearts, they must be void of our own glory."* — **IDL**

> *"The truly humble of heart do not wish to appear humble, but to be humble."* — **Camus**

> *"As Job's patience was enthroned on a dung-hill, so God's mercy is raised upon the wretchedness of man; take away man's misery, and what becomes of God's mercy?"* — **Camus**

### 4. The will of God and abandonment
> *"The indifferent heart is like a ball of wax in the hands of its God, receiving with equal readiness all the impressions of the eternal good pleasure; it is a heart without choice, equally disposed for everything, having no other object of its will than the will of its God, and placing its affection not upon the things that God wills, but upon the will of God who wills them."* — **TLG**

> *"Ask for nothing, refuse nothing, imitate Jesus in His cradle."* — **Camus**

> *"It is so lost and dispersed in the will of God that it appears not, and has no other will than the will of God."* — **TLG** (the death of the will)

### 5. Prayer
> *"Inasmuch as prayer places our understanding in the clearness of the divine light, and exposes our will to the warmth of heavenly love, there is nothing which so purges our understanding of its ignorance and our will of its depraved inclinations; it is the water of benediction, which, when our souls are watered therewith, makes the plants of our good desires revive and flourish, cleanses our souls of their imperfections, and quenches the thirst caused by the passions of our hearts."* — **IDL**

> *"Willing to be in prayer without any other aim than to be in the sight of God according as it shall please him."* — **TLG** (prayer of quiet)

### 6. Love of neighbour and friendship
> *"O Philothea, love everyone with a great love of charity, but have no friendship save with those who can communicate with you in virtuous things; and the more exquisite the virtues are, which are the matter of your intercommunication, the more perfect will your friendship be."* — **IDL**

> *"It seems to me that God has given me to you. I feel more certain of it every day."* — to St Jane de Chantal

### 7. The cross and suffering
> *"Painful things cannot be loved when considered in themselves; but when viewed in their source, i.e., in the divine will and providence which ordains them, they are supremely delightful. Look at tribulations in themselves and they are dreadful; behold them in the will of God and they are love and delight."* — **Beahn**

> *"Love and death are so mingled in the Passion of Our Saviour that we cannot have the one in our heart without the other."* — **TLG**

> Mount Calvary is *"the academy of love."* — **TLG**

### 8. Patience with oneself
> *"Have patience with every one, but especially with yourself."*

> *"When we have committed a fault, let us at once examine our heart… We must then correct ourselves gently and quietly, and not irritate and disturb ourselves still more. Rise up, my heart, my friend, we should say to ourselves, and lift up our thoughts to our Help, and our God. Yes, my dear daughter, we must be charitable to our own soul, and not rebuke it over harshly when we see that the fault it has committed was not fully wilful."* — **Camus**

> *"We must make haste leisurely… He that is hasty with his feet, is in danger of stumbling."*

### 9. Preaching and evangelisation
> *"Do you wish to know how I test the excellence and value of a preacher? It is by assuring myself that those who have been listening to him come away striking their breasts and saying: 'I will do better'; not by their saying: 'Oh how well he spoke, what beautiful things he said!'"* — **Camus**

> *"Our words must be enkindled, not by exaggerated shouting or elaborate gestures, but by our inner love. Words must come from the heart rather than the mouth, for the heart speaks to the heart while the tongue speaks only to the ear."* — **Beahn**

> *"Truth which is not charitable proceeds from a charity which is not true."*

> *"To force the mind to believe is to force it from all belief."* — **CC**

> *"The sole and true rule of right-believing is the Word of God preached by the Church of God."* — **CC**

> *"How shall they preach, says the Apostle, unless they be sent?"* — **CC**

### 10. "Live Jesus" and the heart
> *"To love, or to die! To die and to love! To die to all other love in order to live to Jesus' love, that we may not die eternally, but that living in Thy eternal love, O Saviour of our souls, we may eternally sing, Vive Jésus, Live Jesus. I love Jesus. Live Jesus, Whom I love! I love Jesus, Who lives and reigns for ever and ever. Amen."* — **TLG**, quoted in **Camus**

> *"Man is the perfection of the universe; the spirit is the perfection of man; love, that of the spirit; and charity, that of love."* — **TLG**

> *"It is better to fear God from love than to love Him from fear."*

> *"All is love's, and in love, for love, and of love."*

## B. Glossary

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Philothea** | "Lover of God" — the addressee of the *Introduction*; any soul desiring devotion |
| **Theotimus** | "One who honours God" — the addressee of the *Treatise*; the soul already advanced |
| **Devotion** | Not a separate virtue: charity at the pitch where it acts *readily, carefully, frequently* — "spiritual nimbleness and vivacity" |
| **Devout humanism** | The Salesian synthesis: an optimistic anthropology + primacy of love, yielding holiness in ordinary life |
| **Composition of place** | In meditation, imaginatively representing the mystery as though present |
| **Spiritual nosegay** | The one or two points from prayer carried through the day, like picked flowers |
| **Direction of intention** | Referring each ordinary action explicitly to God's will, making little actions great |
| **Little virtues** | Humility, gentleness, patience, simplicity — growing "like wild thyme at the foot of the Tree of Life" |
| **Abjection** | One's actual lowliness/humiliation; humility's summit is to *love* it |
| **Liberty of spirit** | Interior freedom that serves from love, not constraint; the opposite of scrupulous bondage |
| **Complacency** | Delighted reception of God's perfections into the soul |
| **Benevolence** | Casting the heart into God, willing him all glory — expressed as praise |
| **Signified will** | God's will made known: commandments, counsels, inspirations → active obedience |
| **Will of good pleasure** | God's providential will in events → submission, indifference |
| **Resignation** | Submission with effort and a residual preference of one's own |
| **Holy indifference** | Loving nothing except for the love of God's will; the ball of wax |
| **Death of the will** | The will so lost in God's will that it has no other — the traveller on the ship |
| **Ecstasy of act and life** | Living wholly governed by charity — superior to ecstasy of prayer or senses |
| **Prayer of quiet** | Faculties gathered around the Beloved; reasoning ceases; the child at the breast |
| **Cordial anthropology** | The heart, not the intellect, as the person's centre; the will as "natural monarchy" |
| **Prapatti / śaraṇāgati** | Śrīvaiṣṇava loving surrender / refuge-taking; Clooney's parallel to holy indifference → [[09 Beyond Compare - Clooney and the Indian Lens]] |
| **Fransalians (MSFS)** | Missionaries of St Francis de Sales, founded by **Fr Peter Mermier at Annecy in 1838**; in India from 1845. (The notebook's "1834" is an error → [[10 Legacy and the Salesian Family]]) |

## C. Bibliography (the notebook's source base)

1. St Francis de Sales, *Introduction to the Devout Life* (1609).
2. St Francis de Sales, *Treatise on the Love of God* (1616).
3. St Francis de Sales, *The Catholic Controversy: Defense of the Faith* (Chablais tracts, 1594–98).
4. Louise Stacpoole-Kenny, *St Francis de Sales: A Biography of the Gentle Saint*.
5. Jean-Pierre Camus, *The Spirit of St Francis de Sales* (contemporary testimony — Camus was Bishop of Belley and his directee).
6. John E. Beahn, *A Man of Good Zeal: A Novel Based on the Life of Saint Francis de Sales* — **fiction**; use for atmosphere and for quotations it transmits, not as an independent source of fact.
7. Francis X. Clooney SJ, *Beyond Compare: St Francis de Sales and Śrī Vedānta Deśika on Loving Surrender to God*.
8. *The Classics Made Simple: An Introduction to the Devout Life*.

> [!warning] Source-critical caution
> Camus is invaluable but hagiographical and written from memory; Stacpoole-Kenny is early-twentieth-century devotional biography; Beahn is a novel. For dates and documented facts prefer the critical Salesian literature (the *Œuvres complètes*, Annecy edition) over all three.
