# 07b — Providence, Peace and the Christian in the World

> [!abstract] Thesis
> This is the note that makes Salesian spirituality **usable by laypeople**: providence in ordinary events, peace as a duty, and concrete counsel for marriage, work, widowhood and authority.

---

## 1. Two images for the layperson

> [!quote]
> Christians in the world are like **mother-of-pearl, which lives in the sea without taking in a drop of salt water**; and like **flies that fly through flames without burning their wings**.

Not withdrawal, not absorption: **immunity through interior orientation**.

## 2. The will of God in daily events

Nothing but sin happens outside the will of God. Therefore embrace **everything** — consolation or toil, health or sickness — so that God's will may be done *"by us, for us, in us, and with us."* The heart is a **ball of wax** before the divine good pleasure → [[06 Treatise on the Love of God]].

## 3. Providence — the berries and the father's hand

> [!quote] The best single counsel he ever gave laypeople
> *"Imitate little children who hold fast to their father with one hand while gathering berries with the other; thus, gathering worldly goods with one hand, hold fast your Heavenly Father with the other."*

Rely entirely on providence for success; **cooperate quietly** with it. The Salesian layperson is neither a fatalist nor a striver.

## 4. Suffering, sickness, death

**Suffering.** *"Painful things cannot be loved when considered in themselves; but when viewed in their source, i.e., in the divine will and providence which ordains them, they are supremely delightful."*

**Sickness.**
- Obey the physician and take the medicine **for the love of God**, remembering the gall Christ tasted.
- Long sicknesses are *"good schools of mercy"* for the caregiver and *"of loving patience"* for the sufferer.
- The rule for distress: ***"Ask for nothing, refuse nothing, desire nothing"*** — rest in God's arms like an infant.

**Death and eternity.** *"Every moment leads us from the cradle to the grave."* Death is naturally formidable; blessed hope and trust in infinite mercy overcome the fear. Meditate the hour of death so that pleasures, vanities and riches *"appear as phantoms and shadows"* — and choose **eternal love over eternal death**.

## 5. Confidence — built on distrust of self

> [!important] The Salesian logic of confidence
> Confidence in God is born of a **profound distrust of ourselves**. Recognising our weakness, we see we have nothing of our own to trust.
> *"The throne of God's mercy is our misery"* — therefore **the greater the misery, the greater the confidence should be.**
> And when we fall: *"It is not surprising that weakness should be weak."*

This is the exact inverse of scrupulosity. Failure is not evidence against you; it is the material mercy works on → [[08 Spiritual Direction, Friendship and the Visitation]].

## 6. Interior peace

Banish **disquietude and eagerness** — *the greatest evil short of sin.* After a fall, do not fret with angry self-reproach; **rise again with peace and tranquillity.**

> [!quote] The instrument image
> Gently **tune the strings of the heart** rather than breaking the instrument in frustration.

## 7. The practice of the presence of God

Four ways (→ full treatment in [[05a Sacramental and Devotional Life]]): God everywhere; God in your heart; God looking down from heaven; imagining Jesus beside you.

Plus the two practices he calls **"the great work of devotion"**: **ejaculatory prayers** and the **spiritual retreat into the heart** — a *"small interior oratory"* accessible in the busiest company.

## 8. Nature and grace

> [!quote]
> *"Grace is so gracious, and so graciously seizes our hearts to draw them, that she no ways offends the liberty of our will."*

Natural moral virtues and good inclinations are a **wild stock**: we must *"engraft on the wild stock of natural inclination shoots of divine charity."* Natural virtue done from a heart full of God's love is **elevated, ennobled, made holy** — not replaced.

> [!tip] Anti-Jansenist in advance
> Grace **perfects and grafts**; it does not destroy or substitute. That single sentence is the difference between Salesian spirituality and the rigorism that will dominate the French seventeenth century after him.

## 9. States of life — concrete counsel

| State | Counsel |
|---|---|
| **Marriage** | A *"great Sacrament"* and the **"nursery of Christianity"** — holy in origin, end and use. Spouses to love one another with a love *"sweet and tranquil, firm and steadfast"* |
| **Work and business** | Manage temporal affairs with a **"gracious care"**, free of the violent, turbulent anxiety of worldly ambition |
| **Widowhood** | Avoid lawsuits and public proceedings unless truly necessary — **holy tranquillity over temporal profit** |
| **Authority and obedience** | Obey ecclesiastical, civil and domestic superiors because God has placed them over us — *"meekly and without repining"*, ideally *"lovingly for the love of Him who made Himself obedient unto death"* |
| **Every state** | *"It is an error, nay rather a heresy"* to exclude devotion from camp, workshop, court or household |

## 10. Four counsels to keep on a card

1. **On social life.** You may *"dance and play, when obliged by the civil request of your companions"* — condescension to others derives from charity.
2. **On correction.** *"More flies are caught with a spoonful of honey than with a hundred barrels of vinegar."*
3. **On mockery.** When the world mocks your devotion, **turn a deaf ear**: *"Let it cry as long as it please, like an owl, to disturb the birds of the day."*
4. **On over-commitment.** *"It is far better to do a few things well than to undertake many good works and leave them half done."* And: *"We turn little actions into great ones when we perform them with a supreme desire to please God."*
