# 09 — *Beyond Compare*: Clooney and the Indian Lens

> [!abstract] What this note is for
> Francis X. Clooney SJ reads the *Treatise on the Love of God* alongside **Śrī Vedānta Deśika's *Śrīmad Rahasyatrayasāra*** ("The Essence of the Three Auspicious Mysteries"). For an Indian theological context this is the single most useful contemporary use of de Sales: it puts **holy indifference** and **prapatti** on the same page.

---

## 1. The method: "beyond comparison"

Clooney insists this is **not** ordinary comparative theology cataloguing similarities and differences. It is a **double reading**: the reader engages both texts simultaneously, moving back and forth, **letting neither dominate or shadow the other**.

Characteristics:
- immersive, not extractive — you submit to the texts;
- the reader becomes **vulnerable** to the transformative purposes both authors intended;
- the goal is a changed reader, not a completed comparison table.

> [!warning] The discipline required
> Clooney's own caution: this is *not* consumerist harvesting of another tradition's insights. The exercise is spiritual before it is academic.

## 2. The shared core: loving surrender

| | **Vedānta Deśika (Śrīvaiṣṇava)** | **Francis de Sales (Catholic)** |
|---|---|---|
| Name | ***prapatti* / *śaraṇāgati*** — taking refuge | **Holy indifference**, submission to the will of good pleasure |
| Act | Radical, **irrevocable** self-giving; life reshaped around Nārāyaṇa and the Goddess Śrī at its centre | The heart placed wholly at God's disposal |
| What is surrendered | The burden of **autonomous self-protection** | Every preference except the will of God |
| Image | (the refuge-taking devotee) | **"A ball of wax in the hands of its God"** |
| Scope | All events accepted from the protector | All events — even suffering and death — with equanimity |

Clooney identifies **loving surrender as the core value uniting the two texts**: both demand total abandonment of the self into divine hands.

## 3. Grace and human effort — parallel balancing acts

Both agree the task of self-liberation exceeds human power; both therefore require **total dependence on divine grace**; both refuse to let that dependence abolish human freedom.

- **De Sales:** grace does not overpower liberty. It ***"entices the heart"***, a "holy violence" leaving the will free to consent or resist.
- **Deśika:** the Lord's grace is absolutely prior and sufficient — yet **human action is required as a "pretext"** for God's saving intervention. The devotee must actually choose to seek a protector and confess helplessness.

> [!important] Where the interest lies
> Not that they say the same thing — they do not. It is that **two traditions with very different metaphysics arrive at structurally identical problems**: how to say "grace does everything" without making the person a puppet. Deśika's *"pretext"* and de Sales's *"enticement"* are two solutions to one problem.

## 4. Rhetoric — the deepest divergence

| | **De Sales** | **Deśika** |
|---|---|---|
| Clooney's figure | **"A great river"** of narrative | **"A laser piercing"** through darkness |
| Aim | Inflame the heart | Absolute intellectual clarity, dispelling illusion |
| Means | Analogies from nature and romantic love; dramatic exemplars (Mary Magdalene, Francis of Assisi); intimate address — *"O Theotimus"* | Crisp analytic discourse; dense anthologies of scriptural citation; word-by-word exegesis (e.g. of the **Dvaya Mantra**) |
| Form | Continuous prose flow | Strategic **alternation of prose and poetry**, Tamil and Sanskrit, forcing different imaginative registers |

> [!tip] Use this in an essay on inculturation
> The **content** converges (surrender, grace, freedom); the **rhetoric** diverges sharply. That is a precise argument against equating inculturation with translating doctrine, and for taking *form* — how a text works on a reader — as itself theological.

## 5. Reading across boundaries: what happens to the reader

- Deep engagement with another tradition's text alongside one's own puts the reader in a **"vulnerable, fruitful learning state."**
- It is **unsettling**: one is distanced from the exclusive safety of the home tradition.
- It is enriching: the words, images and exemplars of both traditions are woven into a single complex interior world.

Clooney aligns this with **Scriptural Reasoning** — but relocates the dialogue: it is **interior, within the individual reader**, before it is social conversation between communities.

> [!quote] The strong claim
> A Catholic can profoundly appropriate Śrīvaiṣṇava riches — **even uttering the Dvaya Mantra as their own prayer** — while remaining fully Catholic. The outcome is a rare kind of believer: **deeply rooted in one tradition, fearlessly open to the other.**

## 6. For Indian theological work

> [!note] Honest limit
> Clooney's book does not itself use the phrase "Indian Christian spirituality", and the notebook contains no Indian-Christian appropriation of de Sales beyond this. The applications below are **inference from the sources, not quotation.**

Lines worth developing:
1. **Bhakti and *douceur*.** Salesian *cordial* anthropology (the heart as the seat of the person, love as the will's first affection) is structurally close to bhakti's centring on the heart — a more natural bridge into Indian religious sensibility than a scholastic anthropology of intellect and will.
2. **Prapatti as a lens on holy indifference.** Reading the *Treatise* through *śaraṇāgita* recovers the *relational* character of indifference: it is not stoic apathy but **refuge-taking in a person**. This is a useful correction to a Western misreading of Bk 9.
3. **The universal call and caste/vocation.** *"Each according to his kind and vocation"* needs care in an Indian context: read carelessly it can sanctify a hierarchical status quo. De Sales's own use is emancipatory — devotion is **denied to no state** — and that is the sense to preserve.
4. **Gentleness as a missionary method.** The Chablais precedent (→ [[03 The Chablais Mission and The Catholic Controversy]]) — refusing coercion, insisting that affection precedes conviction — is directly usable in a pluralist and often hostile Indian public square.
