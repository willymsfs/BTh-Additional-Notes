# 02a — Portrait, Family and Death

> [!abstract] Why this note exists
> [[02 Life and Biography - Chronology]] gives the dated skeleton. This gives the **man** — the face, the family, the temper, the jokes, and the death. Preachers and formators need this note more than the chronology.

---

## 1. Physical portrait

- Born a beautiful child with **golden curls**, shorn — despite his tears — at the **tonsure aged eleven**.
- As a youth: **straight, slender, handsome; kind blue eyes; a winning smile.**
- As bishop: majestic and dignified; **close-cropped round beard, no moustache**; somewhat full lips often holding a hint of a **sarcastic but good-humoured smile**.
- Grew noticeably **stouter** with age; witnesses report his face often shining with a radiant, unearthly light.

## 2. Childhood and the Abbé Déage

At **La Roche**, an aristocratic distaste for the noisy games of his peers left him somewhat lonely and isolated. At **Annecy** the character deepened: he grasped that natural superiority carries **responsibility for others**, and began a gracious, active interest in his companions.

His tutor **Abbé Déage** accompanied him for years — irritable, petulant, given to **brusque dictation and scolding**, and still treating Francis like a schoolboy when he was a Prince-Bishop.

> [!important] The first school of gentleness
> Francis submitted always with unfailing gentleness and sweet humour — and **wept bitterly when the old man died**, remembering him as the one who had taught him the *Our Father*. Twenty years of daily irritation borne without retaliation: the little virtues were learnt on a difficult tutor long before they were written down.

## 3. Padua — the near-death

The predestination temptation plus severe austerities reduced him to **a skeleton**. His life hung so precariously that he was **given the Last Sacraments**. He recovered after surrendering his will to God, guided by **Fr Scupoli** (author of *The Spiritual Combat*) and **Fr Possevin**.

> [!tip] Note the two spiritual fathers
> Scupoli's *Spiritual Combat* is a lifelong companion book for him. The Salesian doctrine of temptation — never dispute with the enemy, act by the contrary virtue — descends directly from this Paduan crisis and its cure → [[07a The Virtues and the Spiritual Combat]].

## 4. Family

Siblings: brothers **Galloys, Jean-François, Louis, Bernard, James**; sisters **Gasparde** and **Jeanne**.

> [!quote] His own joke about the family
> **Jean-François** was the **vinegar** — sharp and pungent of tongue. **Louis** was the **salt** — wisdom. And he himself was the **oil** — for his love of gentleness.

**Jean-François** became a zealous priest and was made **Coadjutor of Geneva (Bishop of Chalcedon)** to help Francis in his decline. Francis's own division of labour: Jean-François takes **the active part of Martha**, leaving Francis **the contemplative part of Mary**.

### His mother, Françoise de Boisy

- **Dedicated him to God before his birth.**
- When his father angrily refused to fund the dangerous Chablais mission, she **secretly supplied money and linen**.
- Francis was **her spiritual director**; she called him the guardian angel of the family.
- **1610** — shortly after making a fervent retreat with him, she was struck by **apoplexy** while dressing. Francis rushed to her. Blind and paralysed, she kissed his hand and embraced him:

> [!quote]
> *"It is my son and my Father."*

He wept abundantly, and sealed the grief with perfect resignation to God's will. (Note the pattern: Salesian indifference **does not suppress the tears** — it accompanies them.)

## 5. The temper — and the gallstones

Naturally lively and **angry**; a heroic lifelong battle to master it.

> [!warning] The autopsy
> After his death the doctors found his **gall turned into some three hundred small stones, like a rosary** — read by contemporaries as the physical record of fifty years of violent self-restraint.

Whether or not one accepts the physiology, keep the image: **Salesian sweetness had a somatic price.** He is not a placid man who happened to be nice.

### Two incidents

- An **irate creditor** furiously abused him and demanded money. Francis stayed entirely serene, saying that if the man **plucked out one of his eyes, he would look affectionately at him with the other**.
- He welcomed critics as allies against his own self-love → [[08 Spiritual Direction, Friendship and the Visitation]].

## 6. The wit

- A **Huguenot** rudely demanded whether the splendid regal carriage he rode in was his own. Francis replied he was **far too poor to own a carriage** — the carriage and the liveried servants belonged to the **King of France**.
- He joked with **Camus, Bishop of Belley**, that it was comical for Geneva to scold Belley for saying Mass too slowly while Belley scolded Geneva for saying it too quickly.

## 7. Princes

| Prince | Relation |
|---|---|
| **Henry IV of France** | Called him *"the phoenix of prelates"*; repeatedly offered rich French sees. Refused: *"I have married a poor wife, and I cannot desert her for a rich one."* |
| **Charles Emmanuel, Duke of Savoy** | Initially strained — the Duke ignored Francis and unjustly **exiled his brothers**. Francis confronted him boldly at **Turin**; the Duke reversed completely and became a steadfast friend and patron. Later offered him the **Archbishopric of Turin**; refused again. |

> [!important] The pattern
> Gentleness is not deference. He faced down a duke over an injustice to his family, and refused two crowns of preferment. **Salesian *douceur* has a spine**; that is what distinguishes it from mere agreeableness.

## 8. The death at Lyons (December 1622)

- Travelled to **Lyons** with the Duke to meet **Louis XIII**.
- Refused the nobles' luxurious lodgings; chose the **cold, smoky, draughty gardener's room** at the Visitation monastery.
- Exhausted by ceaseless preaching, confessions and court duties → **apoplexy**.
- Treatment: doctors repeatedly applied **red-hot irons to his head and the nape of his neck, burning to the bone**, to rouse him from stupor. He made **no complaint**, intoning the *Te Deum* and reciting psalms through it.

> [!quote] Last words
> *"Advesperavit, et inclinata est jam dies"* — **"It is towards evening, and the day is now far spent"** (Lk 24:29).
> His last word on earth: **"Jesus."**

**28 December 1622** — the **Feast of the Holy Innocents**. Aged 55.

> [!tip] Homiletic note
> The dying quotation is the Emmaus line — *stay with us*. A bishop who spent his life persuading rather than compelling, dies quoting the request two disciples made to a stranger they could not yet name. Then: *Jesus*.

- Q:: Which two spiritual masters guided him at Padua? A:: Fr Scupoli (*The Spiritual Combat*) and Fr Possevin
- Q:: How did he describe his brothers Jean-François and Louis, and himself? A:: Vinegar, salt, and oil
- Q:: What did his mother say on her deathbed? A:: "It is my son and my Father"
- Q:: What was found at his autopsy, and what was it taken to prove? A:: About 300 gallstones like a rosary — the cost of fifty years of restraining anger
