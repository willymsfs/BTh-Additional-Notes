## The doctrine in one paragraph

God is love, and has planted in every heart a natural inclination to love him — a spark under ashes that grace **entices** rather than overrides. Therefore holiness is not a monastic monopoly but the vocation of every state of life, and **devotion** is simply charity made prompt, ready and joyful. The road is not extraordinary austerity but the **little virtues** — humility, gentleness, patience, simplicity — practised in the **present duty** with a **directed intention**, by the **will** rather than the feelings, so that dryness is no obstacle but an increase of merit. Its mystical term is **holy indifference**: the heart a ball of wax before the will of God's good pleasure, its self-will dead, its love expressed not in rapture but in the **ecstasy of act and life**: *Live Jesus.*

## The sacramental floor (don't omit this in an essay on "gentleness")

Daily **Mass** ("the sun of spiritual exercises") · **communion** weekly, up to daily under direction, never beyond a month · **confession** weekly · **morning exercise** and **evening examen** · **aspirations** and the retreat into the heart — *"the great work of devotion"* · daily **spiritual reading** · **annual retreat** (the clock cleaned and oiled). → [[05a Sacramental and Devotional Life]]

## Six distinctions that carry marks

1. Devotion ≠ devotions; devotion = **charity's promptitude**.
2. **Signified will** (obedience) vs **will of good pleasure** (indifference).
3. **Resignation** (submits with residual preference) vs **holy indifference** (no preference but God's will).
4. **Meditation** (bees gathering) vs **contemplation** (relishing the honey).
5. **Ecstasy of prayer/senses** vs **ecstasy of act and life** (the true test).
6. Charity toward **persons** vs severity toward **claims** (the Chablais method).
7. **Feeling** a temptation vs **consenting** to it — no consent, no sin.
8. **Holy zeal** (charity's ardour, corrects in sweetness) vs **bitter zeal** (passion disguised as righteousness).
9. **Holy sadness** (repentance, compassion) vs **melancholy** (a spiritual danger to be treated).
10. **Loving abjection** vs **neglecting one's good name** — charity requires care of reputation.

## Five images

wax ball · statue in the niche · child at the breast · deaf lutenist · bees (+ eagle: more sight than flight)

## Five quotations to have verbatim

1. *"Devotion is no other thing than a spiritual nimbleness and vivacity, by means of which charity works in us."*
2. *"It is an error, nay rather a heresy, to wish to banish the devout life from the army, from the workshop…"*
3. *"More flies are caught with a spoonful of honey than with a hundred barrels of vinegar."*
4. *"The indifferent heart is like a ball of wax in the hands of its God."*
5. *"Have patience with every one, but especially with yourself."*

# Flashcards (spaced repetition)

- Q:: Devotion, defined? A:: Charity at the pitch where it works "readily and heartily" — spiritual nimbleness and vivacity
- Q:: Addressees of the two great books? A:: Philothea (*Introduction*) and Theotimus (*Treatise*)
- Q:: Five parts of the *Introduction*? A:: Purgation; prayer and sacraments; virtues; temptations; renewal
- Q:: Four movements of the twelve books of the *Treatise*? A:: Foundations (1–4); the two exercises (5); prayer (6–7); the two wills (8–9); charity's sovereignty and counsels (10–12)
- Q:: Two exercises of holy love? A:: Complacency and benevolence
- Q:: The two wills of God? A:: Signified will; will of good pleasure
- Q:: The wax-ball image teaches what? A:: Holy indifference
- Q:: The deaf lutenist teaches what? A:: Serving God faithfully in spiritual dryness, for his pleasure alone
- Q:: Why are dry acts worth more? A:: Dried roses give the stronger scent
- Q:: What is the ecstasy of act and life? A:: Living so governed by charity that "Christ liveth in me" — the true proof of love
- Q:: Root of scruples, per de Sales? A:: A cunning self-esteem
- Q:: The apologetic question at the heart of *The Catholic Controversy*? A:: "Who sent you?" — the question of legitimate mission
- Q:: His rule of faith in one line? A:: "The Word of God preached by the Church of God"
- Q:: Whom did he debate at Geneva in 1597? A:: Theodore Beza
- Q:: What was the Visitation designed for? A:: Women weak, ill or elderly; mortification of the will substituted for that of the flesh
- Q:: What does Clooney pair with holy indifference? A:: Prapatti / śaraṇāgati in Vedānta Deśika
- Q:: Doctor of the Church — when, by whom? A:: 1877, Pius IX
- Q:: Patron of writers and journalists — when, why? A:: 1923, Pius XI; the Chablais tracts
- Q:: MSFS — founder, date, place? A:: Fr Peter Mermier, 1838, Annecy (under Bishop Rey); India 1845
- Q:: Which Vatican II text carries the Salesian thesis of universal holiness? A:: *Lumen Gentium* ch. V, nn. 39–42
- Q:: What does he call the Mass? A:: "The sun of spiritual exercises"
- Q:: What does he call "the great work of devotion"? A:: Ejaculatory prayers/aspirations and the spiritual retreat into the heart
- Q:: Three marks of a genuine inspiration? A:: Perseverance; peace and gentleness of heart; humble obedience
- Q:: How are small daily temptations conquered? A:: Not by disputing but by an act of the contrary virtue, or turning to Christ crucified
- Q:: The Visitation coat of arms? A:: Heart pierced with two arrows, crowned with thorns, surmounted by a cross, engraved with the names Jesus and Mary
- Q:: How did he describe his brothers and himself? A:: Jean-François vinegar, Louis salt, himself oil
- Q:: What was found at his autopsy? A:: Some 300 gallstones like a rosary — read as the cost of restraining his anger for fifty years
- Q:: What did his mother say when dying? A:: "It is my son and my Father"
- Q:: His last Latin words? A:: "Advesperavit, et inclinata est jam dies" (Lk 24:29)

# Essay and exam questions

## Short answer (5 marks)
1. Explain the Salesian definition of devotion and distinguish it from three false devotions.
2. What is "direction of intention", and what problem does it solve?
3. Distinguish resignation from holy indifference, with the wax-ball image.
4. Why is the "ecstasy of act and life" superior to the ecstasy of prayer?
5. Why is St Francis de Sales patron of journalists?

## Essay (15–20 marks)
1. *"It is an error, nay rather a heresy, to wish to banish the devout life…"* — Assess the claim that St Francis de Sales anticipated the universal call to holiness of *Lumen Gentium* 39–42. Ground your answer in the *Introduction to the Devout Life* and the conciliar text, and say what, if anything, the Council added.
2. **"Gentleness is not a temperament but an ascesis."** Discuss with reference to Camus's testimony and the Chablais mission.
3. Expound the doctrine of the two wills of God in Books 8–9 of the *Treatise*, and evaluate holy indifference as an ideal of Christian maturity. Does it risk passivity?
4. Compare Salesian optimism about human nature with the Calvinist and Jansenist accounts he opposed. What is at stake pastorally?
5. Analyse the Salesian method of controversy: how does he combine calling his opponents "brothers" with calling their ministers "impostors" — and is the combination coherent?
6. *"The heart speaks to the heart while the tongue speaks only to the ear."* Construct a Salesian theology of preaching and assess its adequacy for the contemporary pulpit.
7. Using Clooney's *Beyond Compare*, evaluate holy indifference and *prapatti* as parallel doctrines of loving surrender. What does the comparison illuminate — and what does it distort?
8. Discuss the friendship of Francis de Sales and Jane de Chantal as a *locus theologicus* for a Christian theology of affection.
9. *"Disquietude is the greatest evil that can befall the soul, except sin."* Expound the Salesian treatment of anxiety, sadness and melancholy, and assess its value for contemporary pastoral care.
10. **"Grace no ways offends the liberty of our will."** Examine the Salesian account of nature and grace — the wild stock and the graft — and contrast it with the Jansenist tendency that followed him in France.
11. Salesian devotion is often reduced to a mood. Show, from his sacramental and devotional discipline, that it is a **structured regime**, and argue what is lost when the structure is dropped.
12. Expound the three marks of a genuine inspiration and evaluate them as a rule of discernment alongside the Ignatian rules.

## Indian-context prompts
1. Does the Salesian doctrine "each according to his kind and vocation" support or subvert a caste-ordered account of duty? Argue carefully.
2. How might Salesian *douceur* function as a missionary method in a religiously plural and sometimes hostile Indian public square? Use the Chablais precedent.
3. Salesian cordial anthropology and bhakti: is the heart-centred person a better bridge into Indian religiosity than a scholastic faculty psychology?
