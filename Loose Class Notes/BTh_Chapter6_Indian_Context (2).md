This is the **climax** of the whole book. Chapters I–V built the tools (what theology is, its sources, history, models, criteria). Chapter VI now asks the concrete question:

> **How do we actually do theology *in India* — for Indian realities, cultures, religions, and the poor?**

Four movements:
- **A.** The **ecclesial traditions** in India (the Churches the theologian belongs to).
- **B.** The **cultural, philosophical and religious** realities to engage.
- **C.** The **socio-economic and political** realities to respond to.
- **D.** The **method and task** of theologizing in India + the pioneer Indian theologians.

**Governing idea:** Indian theology is caught between two poles it must hold together — deep **inculturation** (rooting in Indian culture/philosophy) and prophetic **liberation** (responding to poverty, caste, injustice).

## A. THE DIFFERENT ECCLESIAL TRADITIONS IN INDIA

**Specification:** The Catholic Church in India is a communion of **three distinct Individual Churches**:
1. the **Syro-Malabar** Church,
2. the **Syro-Malankara** Church,
3. the **Latin** Church.

*"Ecclesial tradition" = a Church-family with its own rite (way of worship), law, and heritage.*

### 1. The St. Thomas Christians

- **Tradition:** St. Thomas the Apostle arrived in **South India in 52 AD.**
- **Famous description (quote):** *"Hindu in culture, Christian in faith and Syrian in worship."*
  - *Simple:* Indian in social/cultural life, Christian in belief, Syrian/Chaldean in liturgy.
- **Why "Syrian in worship"?** A colony of Christian immigrants came from **Persia in the 4th century** to Malabar, bringing their clergy and bishops; gradually **Persian Bishops** governed the St. Thomas Christians.
- **16th-century fracture:** **Portuguese missionaries** interfered, trying to *"Romanize and latinize this ancient Church,"* which the native Christians **resented.** This began successive divisions. Today the heritage comprises **eight Oriental Churches.**

**Key sub-Churches:**

| Church | Key facts (with dates) |
|--------|------------------------|
| **Syro-Malabar Church** | *"The most ancient Church in India,"* **always maintained communion with Rome**; ~5 million members, mainly Kerala; now a Major Archiepiscopal Church with its own Synod |
| **Syro-Malankara Catholic Church** | Formed **1930**, when a group from the **Jacobite Church**, led by **Mar Ivanios** and **Mar Theophilus**, reunited with the Catholic Church |
| **Mar Thoma Church** | Reform Church born of the **Anglican/CMS** contact; **1836** Synod of Mavelikkara (Mar Dionysius IV severed missionary ties); **Mar Abraham Malpan** led radical reform; episcopal line from **Mar Athanasius Mathew (1843)** |

### 2. The Latin Rite

- Originated from **Portuguese missions** — **Franciscans** around **1518.**
- The Jesuit **St. Francis Xavier** arrived in **Goa in 1542**; revered as the *"Apostle of Latin Christianity in India."*

### 3. Protestant Churches

- **Anglicans, Lutherans, Methodists** arrived in the colonial era.
- Ecumenical unions produced:
  - **Church of South India (CSI)** — **1947**
  - **Church of North India (CNI)** — **1970**
- *Ecumenical = concerned with reuniting divided Christian Churches.*

**Specification — why this matters for theology:** the Indian theologian does not start from a blank page but from a **rich, ancient, plural Christian heritage** (Thomas Christians older than most European Churches) that is already deeply Indian in culture.

## B. CULTURAL, PHILOSOPHICAL AND RELIGIOUS REALITIES

### 1. The Indian Worldview — Cosmotheandric

- India possesses a *"cosmotheandric understanding of reality."*
  - *cosmos (world) + theos (God) + andros (human)* → world, God, and human held together **without dichotomy** (no split into "sacred vs secular").

### 2. The Four Goals of Life — Purusharthas

Human life aims at four goals (quote):
> *"wealth (artha), pleasure (kama), righteousness (dharma) and final liberation (moksha)."*

| Sanskrit | Meaning | Example |
|----------|---------|---------|
| *artha* | wealth, security | earning for the family |
| *kama* | pleasure, love, beauty | enjoying art, relationships |
| *dharma* | righteousness, duty | living ethically |
| *moksha* | final liberation | spiritual freedom, the ultimate goal |

**Key Indian insight:** these are **integrated**, not opposed — lower goals serve the higher; together they make a whole human life.

### 3. The Six Schools of Philosophy

Classical systems (quote): *"Nyaya, Vaiseshika, Sankya, Yoga, Mimamsa and Vedanta."*

| School | Simple meaning |
|--------|----------------|
| **Nyaya** | logic — right reasoning as the path to salvation |
| **Vaiseshika** | dualist — matter and soul are distinct realities |
| **Sankhya** | dualist — two principles: *prakrti* (matter) and *purusha* (consciousness) |
| **Yoga** | discipline/meditation for **union with the Absolute** |
| **Mimamsa** | Vedic exegesis — right interpretation of sacred texts |
| **Vedanta** | the culmination — systematic teaching of the Upanishads |

### 4. Vedanta and Advaita — Sankara (9th century AD)

- **Sankara (9th century AD)** popularized ***Advaita Vedanta*** (non-dualism).
- *Advaita = "not two"; only one ultimate reality.*
- Teaching (quote): *"only the Absolute is wholly and truly real, that is called Brahman."*
- By **selfless action** (*nishkama karma*) and **meditation**, a person attains wisdom (*jnana*) to peel away the illusions of *karma* and realize their identity as (quote):
  > *"true Being, Consciousness and Bliss (sat, chit and ananda = saccidananda)."*

**Term explainers (from scratch):**
- *Brahman* = the one absolute Reality behind everything (like the ocean; all things are its waves).
- *Jnana* = transformative wisdom/realization (not mere information).
- *Karma* = action and its consequences that "veil" the true self.
- *Saccidananda* = *sat* (Being) + *chit* (Consciousness) + *ananda* (Bliss) — a three-fold name for ultimate Reality.

### 5. Bhakti — Ramanuja

- **Ramanuja** later **modified** Sankara's strict non-dualism, interpreting the Absolute as a (quote):
  > *"personal deity worthy of human devotion, praise and love."*
- *Bhakti = the path of loving personal devotion to a personal God.*

**Specification — two great streams the theologian can draw on:**

| Stream | Ultimate is... | Salvation by... | Christian use |
|--------|----------------|-----------------|---------------|
| **Advaita** (Sankara) | Impersonal Brahman | Wisdom (*jnana*) | Christ as ultimate self-realization; mysticism |
| **Bhakti** (Ramanuja) | Personal God | Loving devotion | Trinity as relationship; personal love of God |

## C. SOCIO-ECONOMIC AND POLITICAL REALITIES

Indian theology must respond to *"glaring contradictions"* — a *"Mixed Economy"* that produced a rich middle class alongside a **widening gulf of poverty.**

**Key realities the theologian must address:**

1. **Poverty & Inequality** — the *"Mixed Economy"* (between socialism and capitalism) created a *"rich middle class and the consequent widening gulf between the rich and the poor."* Globalization and mechanization leave the poor *"unwanted and unemployed."*

2. **Caste** — though *"constitutionally and legally abolished, the system remains practically unchanged"*; class and caste discrimination are linked (the middle class largely from upper castes).

3. **Political Communalism (Hindutva)** — India's secular character is *"threatened by the Hindutva movement,"* a *"very dangerous ideology"* seeking a *"Hindu Rashtra"* (Hindu nation) where minorities are *"reduced to second class citizens."*
   - *Communalism = hostility/politics based on religious-community identity. Hindutva = the ideology of Hindu-nation nationalism.*

4. **Women** — face *"unimaginable forms of violence and discrimination,"* including **female foeticide, dowry deaths,** and marginalization.

5. **Dalits & Tribals (Adivasis)** — Dalits (formerly *"untouchables"* or *panchamas* = "fifth caste") face social/economic segregation; **Adivasis/Tribals** (Scheduled Tribes ~8% of population) are *"uprooted from their habitats due to industry and deforestation."*
   - *Adivasi = "original inhabitant"; India's indigenous tribal peoples. Panchama = "fifth," those placed outside/below the four-varna caste order.*

### Theological Responses to These Realities

- **Indian Theology of Liberation** — unlike Latin America, it must account for **religious pluralism.**
  - **Sebastian Kappen** pioneered a *"new Indian Christology or Jesuology,"* placing Jesus in continuity with Indian *"socio-religious movements of dissent, contestation and revolt"* against sterile ritualism.
  - **Paul D. Devanandan** and **M. M. Thomas** (Protestant) linked the Gospel to the **Indian renaissance**, viewing Christ as the prototype for a *"new humanity."*
  - **M. Amaladoss** — noted author on **Asian liberation theology.**

- **Dalit Theology** — a *"Dalit version of liberation theology"* that **rejects the upper-caste philosophical categories** of traditional (Brahminic) Indian theology.
  - The *"Crucified and Risen Jesus stood at the centre"*: the **Crucified Jesus** gives power to **revolt** against oppression; the **Risen Jesus** provides **hope.**

- **Women's Voice (Feminist Theology)** — addressing a *"heavily patriarchal society,"* women theologians **Vandana Mataji** and **Sara Grant** drew from the **Ashram tradition** to call for a *"theology from the heart"* balancing male intellectualism with female intuition and care.

**Specification — the Indian difference:** Indian liberation theology cannot simply copy Latin America. It must integrate **class analysis (poverty) + caste analysis (Dalit) + cultural/religious pluralism** simultaneously.

## D. THE METHOD AND TASK OF THEOLOGIZING IN INDIA

Three dynamic methods root Christianity in India:

### 1. Inculturation
- *"Inculturation has been always a watchword in Indian theology."*
- True inculturation is **not mere translation** of Western concepts; it is a *"natural process"* where the Church becomes fully involved in the *"struggles, languages, and 'smaller cultures'"* of marginalized groups.
- *Inculturation = letting the Gospel take genuine root in a culture, taking flesh in its forms — not importing a foreign package.*

### 2. Inter-religious Dialogue
- Shift **from debating dogmas to a *"dialogue of life."***
- People of different faiths gather to tackle **concrete problems together** — *"poverty, environmental issues, and religious fundamentalism."*
- *Dialogue of life = living, cooperating, and solving real problems side-by-side with people of other faiths, rather than only arguing doctrine.*

### 3. The Christian Ashram Movement
- Pioneered by both Protestants and Catholics.
- Ashrams integrate the classical Hindu paths of ***nishkama karma*** (selfless action), ***jnana*** (wisdom), and ***bhakti*** (devotion) into a *"spiritual-contemplative approach."*
- Serve as vital **meeting points for inter-religious dialogue.**
- *Ashram = a place of simple community life centred on prayer, meditation, and spiritual discipline (from the Hindu tradition).*

## KEY PIONEER INDIAN THEOLOGIANS (Names & Contributions)

| Theologian | Tradition | Core contribution & exact phrase |
|-----------|-----------|----------------------------------|
| **Brahmabandhab Upadhyaya** | Catholic, Vedanta | Wore the *"saffron cloth and became a sannyasi"*; argued Indian Christians must use *Vedanta* to translate the faith, as the early Fathers used Greek philosophy |
| **A. J. Appasamy** | Protestant, Bhakti | Used South Indian *Bhakti*; famously used *Saccidananda* (Being, Consciousness, Bliss) to explain the **Trinity** |
| **Pandipeddi Chenchaiah** | Protestant | Christ as the *"new creation"*; humans become *"other Christs"* through an integral Yoga releasing the Holy Spirit — *"the new cosmic energy"* |
| **Vengal Chakkarai** | Protestant | Began theology with the **direct experience of Christ**, explaining Him as an *avatara* (incarnation) |
| **Swami Abhishiktananda** (Henri Le Saux) | Catholic, Ashram | Champion of the Ashram movement; advocated a *"Christian Advaita"* (non-duality); God experienced inwardly in *"the cave of the heart"* |
| **Sebastian Kappen** | Catholic, Liberation | *"new Indian Christology or Jesuology"*; Jesus in Indian *"movements of dissent, contestation and revolt"* |
| **Paul D. Devanandan & M. M. Thomas** | Protestant | Linked Gospel to the Indian renaissance; Christ as prototype of a *"new humanity"* |
| **M. Amaladoss** | Catholic | Asian liberation theology |
| **Vandana Mataji & Sara Grant** | Catholic, Ashram/Feminist | *"theology from the heart"* from the Ashram tradition |

**Term explainers:**
- *Sannyasi = a renouncer who has left worldly life for God (wears saffron).*
- *Avatara = a "descent"; God taking visible bodily form (Hindu concept).*
- *Christian Advaita = using the non-dual Vedantic framework to express the Christian experience of union with God.*
