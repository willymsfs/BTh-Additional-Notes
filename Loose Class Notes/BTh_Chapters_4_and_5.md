# CHAPTER IV — SELECTED MODELS OF THEOLOGY

## OVERVIEW

Chapter III traced *history*. Chapter IV zooms in on **five concrete "models"** — five different, legitimate ways of actually *doing* theology. Each model is anchored in a real theologian and a real cultural context.

**The point of the chapter:** to show that theology is genuinely **plural in method** — poetry, system, anthropology, liturgy, and praxis are all valid roads, provided each keeps the single focus on Christ.

| # | Model | Theologian (dates) | Tradition/Context | One-line idea |
|---|-------|--------------------|--------------------|---------------|
| 1 | Syriac-Semitic | **St. Ephraem** (c. 306–373) | Early un-Hellenized Syriac | Theology as **poetry** |
| 2 | Scholastic | **Thomas Aquinas** (1225–1274) | Golden Age of Scholasticism | Theology as **sacred science** |
| 3 | Theological Anthropology | **Karl Rahner** (1904–1984) | Modern Catholic / Vatican II | Theology through the **human person** |
| 4 | Liturgical Experience | **Alexander Schmemann** (1921–1983) | Orthodox Eastern Church | Theology as **worship articulated** |
| 5 | Critical Reflection on Praxis | Liberation theology (1970s) | Latin America | Theology as **action for the poor** |

## MODEL 1 — A SYRIAC-SEMITIC APPROACH: ST. EPHRAEM (c. 306–373)

**Context / Specification:**
- Ephraem represents the ancient **Syriac-Semitic tradition** — a view of Christianity in its early, *"unhellenized"* form, **before** it adopted Greek philosophical categories.
- *Un-Hellenized = not yet shaped by Greek (Hellenic) philosophy; still using Semitic, biblical, poetic thought-forms.*

**Key Concept & Method:**
- Theology is *"poetry,"* not dogmatic definition.
- Defining God is *"blasphemous"* — an attempt to *"tie down and restrict the Limitless One."*
- His method proceeds *"by way of paradox and symbol"* and requires an attitude of *"love and wonder."*
- He rejected the *"uninvolved"* study of God, because that *"tends to reduce God to an object"* and makes theology a *"one-way street: from the mind of the theologian to the object of inquiry — God."*

**Concrete Example:**
- Ephraem views **nature and Sacred Scripture** as two complementary *"realms of revelation."*
- Revelation *"is not limited to the pages of Sacred Scripture, but is encountered everywhere in the universe."*
- The physical world contains symbols that are instances of *"divine self-manifestation,"* perceived through the *"inner eye of faith."*

**Simple takeaway:** For Ephraem, you do not *dissect* God with logic; you *contemplate* God through symbol, paradox, and hymn — with love and wonder.

---

## MODEL 2 — A SCHOLASTIC MODEL: THOMAS AQUINAS (1225–1274)

**Context / Specification:**
- Aquinas wrote during the *"Golden Age of Scholasticism"* in Western Europe.
- His project: **integrate Aristotelian philosophy with biblical revelation.**

**Key Concept & Method:**
- Theology is a *"sacred science"* (*scientia*).
- Goal: *"faith seeking understanding,"* with philosophy serving as a *"handmaid"* (*ancilla theologiae*) to theology.
- Method: he replaced the personal *"way of discovery (via inventionis)"* with the objective *"way of exposition (via doctrinae)."*
  - *Via inventionis = the a posteriori path of searching-and-finding; via doctrinae = the a priori path of orderly teaching once the truth is possessed.*

**Concrete Example:**
- He organized theology into massive systematic syntheses (*Summae*) using Aristotelian logic and metaphysics to *"logically deduce conclusions."*
- In this system he treats *"of God primarily and of creatures only in so far as they are referable to God."*

**Simple takeaway:** Aquinas builds a vast, ordered cathedral of thought — everything centred on God, reason and faith working together.

---

## MODEL 3 — THEOLOGICAL ANTHROPOLOGY: KARL RAHNER (1904–1984)

**Context / Specification:**
- Rahner was *"a leading figure in the renewal of modern Roman Catholic theology before and during Vatican II."*

**Key Concept & Method:**
- He pioneered *"Theological Anthropology,"* using a *"transcendental"* analysis to examine the *"a priori"* conditions of human acts.
  - *Transcendental method = examining the built-in conditions that make human knowing/acting possible in the first place.*
  - *A priori = prior to experience; the pre-conditions already "in" the human structure.*
- Humans are essentially *"spirit"* and *"awaiters"* of God's word, naturally possessing an *"openness to God."*

**Concrete Example — the "Supernatural Existential":**
- To bridge **nature and grace**, Rahner proposed the *"supernatural existential."*
- *Simple meaning:* God's grace is not *"an alien force dropped from above,"* but is *already present* in human questioning, exploring, and our natural desire for the Infinite.
- Therefore the **Incarnation** is *"the ultimate historical answer to the innate human search for an 'absolute saviour.'"*

**Simple takeaway:** Study the human being deeply enough and you find a built-in openness to God; the Gospel is the answer to a question we already are.

---

## MODEL 4 — ARTICULATION OF THE LITURGICAL EXPERIENCE: ALEXANDER SCHMEMANN (1921–1983)

**Context / Specification:**
- Schmemann writes from the **Orthodox Eastern Churches**, which *"separated from Rome in 1054"* (the East-West Schism).

**Key Concept & Method:**
- Theology is the articulation of a *"lived experience of wholeness"* centred entirely on the **liturgy.**
- Liturgy is *"the revelation, the embodiment and the expression of the Church's faith."*
- Governing adage: *"The norm of prayer is the norm of belief"* (*Lex orandi lex credendi*).

**Concrete Example — "food" and "blessing":**
- God gives the world as a **sacrament**; *"to 'name' a thing is to 'bless' God for it."*
- The Christian is *"the priest, who stands in the centre of the cosmos and unifies it"* — receiving the world and offering it back to God in the liturgy.

**Simple takeaway:** You learn who God is first by **worshipping**; theology then puts into words what the worshipping community has already tasted.

---

## MODEL 5 — CRITICAL REFLECTION ON CHRISTIAN PRAXIS: LIBERATION THEOLOGY (1970s)

**Context / Specification:**
- Emerged in the **1970s in Latin America**, in a context marked by *"utter dependence and oppression for millions of people."*

**Key Concept & Method:**
- Starting point is not abstract truth but *"praxis"* — concrete involvement and commitment to the poor.
  - *Praxis = committed practical action (not mere theory).*
- Demands *"social analysis"* using the social sciences to uncover the causes of oppression.
- Hermeneutical method: *"reading of the Bible from the standpoint of the poor and oppressed."*

**Concrete Example — Exodus:**
- Liberation theologians treat the Old Testament **Exodus** as a *"key paradigmatic text"* — a God who actively **liberates people from unjust political and social systems.**
- Salvation is *"holistic,"* involving *"social freedom, human dignity, and spiritual salvation simultaneously."*

**Simple takeaway:** Theology begins on the ground — in the struggle against injustice — and reads the Bible through the eyes of the poor.

---

## CHAPTER IV — WHY FIVE MODELS? (The Point)

Each model captures something the others under-stress:

| Model | Strength | If taken alone, risk |
|-------|----------|----------------------|
| Ephraem (poetry) | Wonder, mystery, symbol | Vagueness, no precision |
| Aquinas (science) | Order, clarity, reason | Dry rationalism |
| Rahner (anthropology) | Connects to human experience | Can blur nature/grace |
| Schmemann (liturgy) | Rooted in worship | Can seem inward-looking |
| Liberation (praxis) | Justice, real-world impact | Can reduce Gospel to politics |

**Held together:** poetry + system + anthropology + liturgy + praxis = a full-orbed theology, all keeping the single focus on Christ.

# CHAPTER V — THE CRITERIA OF CHRISTIAN THEOLOGICAL REFLECTION

## OVERVIEW

Chapter IV showed **many legitimate models**. That raises the sharp question of Chapter V:

> *"Where does legitimate pluralism end and heterodoxy begin in Roman Catholic theological reflection?"*

If theologies can be so diverse, **how do we tell an authentically Catholic theology from one that has "lost its Catholic character and moorings"?** Chapter V supplies the **criteria** (tests/standards) for that discernment.

Structure:
- **A.** The need for criteria.
- **B.** How different Christian traditions set their criteria (Orthodox / Protestant / Catholic).
- **C.** The Roman Catholic understanding of Church authority (infallibility + gradations of teaching and assent).
- **D.** The eight parameters of the Catholic theological enterprise.

---

## A. THE NEED FOR CRITERIA

**Core reasoning:**
- Christian theology reflects on **God's definitive self-communication**, so it involves a fundamental *"givenness."*
- The ultimates of Christianity are *"not negotiable under popular pressure."*
- Criteria are needed to stop theology from **betraying its past** or **mutating into heterodoxy.**
  - *Heterodoxy = teaching that departs from the accepted/orthodox faith.*

**Key citation — Paul VI, *Evangelii Nuntiandi* (EN 65):**
- There is a real need to encourage theological expression for **different cultures**, but always with the *"indispensable demand that the content of faith be neither impaired nor mutilated."*

**Methodological principle — Roy Eckhart** (*The Theologian at Work*, New York 1969):
- *"a sensible way to discover what an enterprise means is to look at what its practitioners do."*
- *Simple:* to define "Catholic theology," watch what faithful Catholic theologians actually do.

**The precise problem stated (quote):**
> *"how can we discern that a theology has lost its Catholic character and moorings, even though it may claim to be so?"*

---

## B. DIFFERENT CHRISTIAN EMPHASES ON CRITERIA

### 1. The Orthodox Emphasis — Synodality / *Sobornost*
- Emphasizes **"synodality"**: authority *"must emerge out of the communion of Churches, and not as an external principle exercised over the Churches."*
- Core term: ***Sobornost*** = *"communion in an organism"* — organic unity where authority is exercised **within** the Church, not above it.
- For the Orthodox, **tradition** is *"dynamic and living... not attainable except by a certain participation from within, by living in the community of the Church,"* empowered by the Spirit.

*Synodality = walking/deciding together as a body (Greek syn-hodos, "common road").*

### 2. The Protestant Emphasis — *Sola Scriptura* / *Solus Deus*
- Reacting against the hierarchical power of the medieval Church, the Reformers insisted on the *"sovereignty of the Gospel itself over all Church offices."*
- To restore *"God Alone"* (*Solus Deus*), they championed *"Scripture Alone"* (*Sola Scriptura*).
- Scripture is held to have both **material and formal sufficiency.**
  - *Material sufficiency = Scripture contains all truths needed for salvation. Formal sufficiency = Scripture is clear enough to interpret itself.*

### 3. The Roman Catholic Emphasis — Scripture **and** Tradition
- The **Council of Trent** definitively **rejected** *Sola Scriptura*, stating truth and rule *"are contained in the written books and unwritten traditions."*
- **Avery Dulles** lists Catholic objections to the Protestant principle:
  1. *"the Bible did not collect itself"* — the **Church** gathered it;
  2. *"the Bible is not self-interpreting"*;
  3. the Bible itself *"refers to other Christians speaking by the Spirit."*

---

## C. THE ROMAN CATHOLIC UNDERSTANDING OF CHURCH AUTHORITY

**General principle:** authority is exercised *within* a **communion of Churches.** The task of authentically interpreting the Word of God is *"entrusted exclusively to the living teaching office of the Church"* (DV 10).

### 1. Infallibility and the *Sensus Fidei*
- **The whole faithful:** *"the universal body of the faithful, who have received the anointing of the Holy One, cannot be mistaken in belief"* (LG 12).
- **Infallibility is also exercised by:**
  - the **college of bishops** — in an ecumenical council, or when dispersed but agreeing on a definitive teaching;
  - the **Pope** when speaking *ex cathedra*.
- *Ex cathedra = "from the chair" of Peter; a solemn, definitive papal definition on faith or morals.*

### 2. Gradations of Magisterial Teaching and the Assent Owed (Key Table)

| Level | What it is | Assent required |
|-------|-----------|-----------------|
| **1. Definitive Dogma** | Truths **revealed by God**, defined *ex cathedra*, by a council, or by the universal Magisterium | **"Assent of Faith"** |
| **2. Definitive Doctrine** | Irreversible teachings **logically connected** to revealed truths | **"Firm Acceptance"** (accept and hold as true) |
| **3. Non-definitive Authoritative Doctrine** | Authoritative but **not infallible** teachings | **"Religious Submission of Intellect and Will"** |
| **4. Prudential Admonitions** | Pastoral judgments, guidelines, disciplinary actions | Obey *"the spirit of any Church law or disciplinary action"* |

**Specification:** the higher the level, the stronger the assent owed. Not everything the Church says carries the same weight — theologians must weigh statements *"according to their qualitative differences."*

---

## D. THE PARAMETERS OF THE CATHOLIC THEOLOGICAL ENTERPRISE

The book traces **eight core criteria** that authenticate a theology as genuinely Roman Catholic:

1. **Knowability of God** — affirms we **can know God**, rejecting all forms of **agnosticism.**
   - *Agnosticism = the claim that we cannot know whether/what God is.*

2. **Faith and Reason** — insists on the **interplay of both**, rejecting both **Fideism** (Faith Alone) and **Rationalism** (Reason Alone).

3. **Universality** — centres on **Jesus Christ** as the *"one and only Good News"* meant for all humanity.

4. **Communitarian Context** — theology is done **within the Catholic Church**, relying on the community's reception of faith (*sensus fidelium*).

5. **Catholicity in Time** — maintains **continuity with the past**, avoiding **relativism** while allowing genuine **growth in understanding.**
   - *Relativism = the view that there is no stable truth, only shifting opinions.*

6. **The Sacramental Principle** — God's self-communication is *"normally mediated by created realities,"* supremely through the **Christ event.**
   - *Simple: God reaches us through concrete, created things — water, bread, wine, and above all the humanity of Christ.*

7. **Scripture and Tradition** — Revelation is transmitted through **both**, *"situating Scripture firmly within Tradition."*

8. **The Magisterium** — accepts the role of Church authority, requiring theologians to **respectfully receive and weigh** magisterial statements *"according to their qualitative differences."*
