# CHAPTER II — THE SOURCES OF THEOLOGY

## OVERVIEW

Chapter I asked *what* theology is. Chapter II asks: **Where does theology get its material?**

Structure:
1. **Foundations** — Faith and Revelation.
2. **Three Primary Sources** — Scripture, Tradition, Magisterium.
3. **Their mutual relationship** (DV 10).
4. **Additional Resources** — people, culture, other religions, the poor.

**Governing image:** Revelation = the spring; Faith = the cup that receives; Scripture–Tradition–Magisterium = three channels carrying the water; culture/experience/the poor = further streams where the same water flows.

---

## A. FOUNDATIONS OF THEOLOGY: FAITH AND REVELATION

**Foundational statement:** *"The foundations of Christian theology are Faith and Revelation."*

### 1. Two Sides of One Event

- *"distinct and distinguishable"* yet *"inseparably united."*
- *"Revelation is God's self-communication... and faith is the response on the human side."*

**Simple meaning:** A conversation needs a speaker (revelation) and a listener who answers (faith). Revelation without faith is a message no one receives.

### 2. Definition of Revelation (Full Quote)

> *"God's gracious self-disclosure reaching out to humans as an invitation, as well as promise, to participate in God's own life of unfathomable love, mediated to us through persons, nature, history, everyday experience, and, in an ultimate way, in and through God's very Word, Jesus Christ."*

| Phrase | Meaning |
|--------|---------|
| "gracious self-disclosure" | God freely unveils Himself — pure gift (grace) |
| "invitation... and promise" | Revelation calls us in and pledges a future |
| "participate in God's own life" | Goal = sharing God's life, not just facts |
| "mediated through persons, nature, history" | God reaches us through concrete channels |
| "ultimate way... Jesus Christ" | Christ is the **fullness** of revelation |

*Gratuitous = freely given, unearned.*

### 3. Four Models (Dimensions) of Revelation

Revelation is *"a very complex reality."* Four models, all true together:

1. **Revelation as Doctrine** — the *"cognitive contents of revelation as propositions or deposit of truth."* → revelation as **truths to believe** (e.g., "God is Trinity").
2. **Revelation as Presence** — *"a personal encounter with God,"* the *"presence of the living and life-giving God."* → revelation as **meeting a Person**.
3. **Revelation as Experience** — an *"existential experience, which is universal and possible for all human persons."* → God met in the depths of lived existence.
4. **Revelation as History** — a *"universal and public historical event that can be historically established."* → God acts in **real events** (Exodus, life of Jesus).

**Why four?** Each corrects the others: doctrine alone is dry; experience alone is vague; history alone is bare fact.

---

## B. THE THREE PRIMARY SOURCES

### 1. SACRED SCRIPTURE

**a) Inspiration & Inerrancy (DV 11):**
- Written by human authors **under the inspiration of the Holy Spirit**, so *"God can be said to be their author too."*
> *"the books of Scripture must be acknowledged as teaching firmly, faithfully, and without error that truth which God wanted to put into the sacred writings for the sake of our salvation"* (DV 11).
- **Specification:** inerrancy is tied to **saving truth** ("for the sake of our salvation") — not a science/history manual.

**b) Interpretation (DV 12):**
- Because *"God's word is contained in human words,"* Scripture *"needs to be interpreted"* — by studying *"language, literary forms,"* and its unity *"as understood by 'the living tradition of the whole Church.'"* Final authority = *"the judgment of the Church"* (DV 12).
- *Literary form = the type of writing (poetry, parable, law) — read each accordingly.*

**c) The Canon:**
> *"In fixing the Canon, the Church did no more than recognize certain writings as apostolic, and by the same token, recognize them as regulating her life and faith."*
- **Specification:** the Church *recognized*, did not *invent* — Scripture and Tradition working together.

**d) Primary Foundation:**
> *"sacred theology rests on the written word of God, together with sacred tradition, as its primary and perpetual foundation"* (Vatican II).

### 2. SACRED TRADITION

**a) Definition:** *"The living transmission accomplished in the Holy Spirit is called Tradition."*
- Handed down through **liturgy, creeds, and the teachings of the Fathers**.
- **Distinction:** *Tradition* (capital T) = the living apostolic faith; *traditions* (small t) = the customs that carry it.

**b) Apostolic Tradition** = the complete Gospel and foundational experience of the early Church.

**c) Development of Doctrine:**
> developments are *"only explicitations and new understandings of the deposit of faith, never entirely new additions."*
- **Simple:** the **implicit** becomes **explicit** — like a seed becoming a tree. Nothing brand-new is invented.
- *Deposit of faith = the whole treasure of revealed truth entrusted to the Church.*
- *Example:* the full Trinity doctrine took centuries to state, but was already implicit in the New Testament.

### 3. THE MAGISTERIUM (Teaching Office)

**a) Role — servant, not master (DV 10):**
> *"The task of authentically interpreting the word of God, whether written or handed down, has been entrusted exclusively to the living teaching office of the Church."*
> *"This teaching office is not above the word of God, but serves it"* — *"listening to it devoutly, guarding it scrupulously, and explaining it faithfully."*

**b) Collegiality of Bishops:**
> *"The Collegiality of the Bishops implies that all the bishops in the Catholic Church together constitute a single body or collegium... upon which is vested the supreme teaching authority of the Church."*
- *Collegium = a college/body; seen in action at an Ecumenical Council.*

**c) Papal Magisterium:**
- The Pope teaches **infallibly** when speaking *ex cathedra* (*Latin: "from the chair" of Peter — a solemn, definitive pronouncement on faith/morals*).
- Ordinary papal teaching requires *"religious assent of will and intellect."*

**d) Sensus Fidei / Sensus Fidelium:**
- *Sensus fidei* = "sense of the faith," a Spirit-given instinct in each believer; *sensus fidelium* = that instinct in the **whole body** of the faithful.
> *"The body of the faithful as a whole, anointed as they are by the Holy One, cannot err in matters of belief."*
- **Specification:** not opinion-polling; it is the Spirit-given instinct of the People of God as a body.

---

## C. THE MUTUAL RELATIONSHIP (DV 6–10)

The three are **not rivals** but one interlocking reality:
- They flow from *"the same divine wellspring,"* and *"merge into a certain unity and tend toward the same end."*
- *"Sacred Tradition and Sacred Scripture form one sacred deposit of the word of God which is committed to the Church"* (DV 6–9).

**The key summary (DV 10):**
> *"Sacred Tradition, Sacred Scripture and the teaching authority of the Church... are so linked and joined together that one cannot stand without the others, and all together and each in its own way, under the action of the Holy Spirit, contribute effectively to the salvation of souls"* (DV 10).

| Source | Its job | Alone it... |
|--------|---------|-------------|
| Scripture | The written Word | ...opens to endless private readings |
| Tradition | The living handing-on | ...loses its written anchor |
| Magisterium | The authentic interpreter | ...has nothing to guard |

**Image:** a **tripod** — remove any leg and it falls.

---

## D. ADDITIONAL RESOURCES OF THEOLOGY

God is *continually present and active in the world and human history*, so theology listens to further resources:

1. **People & their Experience** — *"Human experience is the meeting-point between God's self-gift and the human response to it"*; *"The people themselves are thus the resources of revelation, and it is through the people that God speaks to us."*

2. **Cultural Resources** — God *"speaks to humans according to their culture of different ages"* (GS 58); *"human cultures are clearly resources of theology."*
   - **India's cosmotheandric vision:** *cosmos* (world) + *theos* (God) + *andros* (human) = a holistic view holding world, God, and humanity together (not "sacred vs secular").

3. **Diverse Religious Traditions** — *"perhaps, the greatest challenge facing our time."* Other religions *"reflect a ray of that truth which enlightens all"* (NA 2); *"The sacred Scriptures of all religions are the heritage of the entire humanity."*
   - **Balance:** genuine resources, **while still affirming Jesus as the source of salvation for all** — openness, not relativism.

4. **Peoples' Movements & the Marginalized** — *"God's word resounds in a special way in the voice of the marginalized"* (human-rights, peasant, Dalit, tribal, women's movements).
   - *Dalit = communities formerly branded "untouchable"; Dalit theology reads the Gospel from their struggle for dignity.*

5. **The Cry of the Poor** — the *"epistemological privilege of the poor"*; *"God speaks today in a very special way through the cry of the poor, their struggles, aspirations, visions and hopes."*
   - *Epistemological = about knowing; the poor "know" the Gospel from an angle the comfortable often miss.*

# CHAPTER III — DEVELOPMENT AND PLURALISM IN THEOLOGY: A HISTORICAL OUTLINE

## OVERVIEW

Chapter II gave the *sources*. Chapter III shows how theology **developed through history** — and how **pluralism** (many valid theologies) has been present **from the very beginning**, even inside the Bible.

**Master thesis of the chapter:** theology keeps a *"delicate balance between maintaining the single focus — Christ, and allowing a legitimate pluralism of expression."* Uniqueness of Christ + multiplicity of expression = the two facets of Christian faith.

**Timeline map:**

| Period | Rough dates | Marker |
|--------|-------------|--------|
| Biblical theologies | OT era → 1st c. AD | Plurality within Scripture itself |
| Patristic (Fathers) | Apostolic → mid-8th c. | Building a theological vocabulary |
| Scholasticism | 800–1500 | University theology; faith + reason |
| Post-Scholastic | 16th–19th c. | Reformation, Trent, Neo-Scholasticism |
| Modern / Contextual | 20th–21st c. | Vatican II; contextual theologies |

---

## A. BIBLICAL THEOLOGIES (Plurality Within Scripture)

**Core specification:** the Bible is not one treatise but *"a library, a collection of books and consequently of theologies."*

### 1. Old Testament Theologies
The OT itself contains multiple theological streams:
> *"a Yahwist Theology, an Elohist Theology, a Priestly Theology, a Deuteronomic Theology and Wisdom Theologies."*

**Why the plurality?** They reflect Israel's changing history — as it *"moved from an agrarian society to a monarchy, from an independent state to becoming a vassal of Assyria, Greece and Rome."*

*Simple:* different historical situations produced different ways of speaking about God — farming society, kingdom, exile, foreign domination.

### 2. New Testament Theologies
- 27 books, written at different times and places.
- The **Gospels** are *"anecdotal rather than biographical"* — place/time indications are *"infrequent, vague and sometimes conflicting."*
- They express *"not merely objective history (what takes place), but the religious significance of these events (what goes on in what takes place)"* — they are *"history interpreted through faith."*

**Plurality of NT theologies (exact quote):**
> *"in the New Testament, it is not illogical to speak of a Matthean theology, a Marcan theology, a Lucan theology and a Johannine theology."*

**Even within one author (Paul):** three Christologies:
- **Ascending Christology** — *"stresses His humanity"* (starts from Jesus the man).
- **Descending Christology** — *"begins from His divinity"* (starts from the eternal Son).
- **Cosmic Christology** — *"linking Him to the entire universe."*

*Christology = the study of the person and work of Christ.*

**The governing balance (exact quote):**
> *"the theologies in the Bible kept a delicate balance between maintaining the single focus - Christ, and allowing a legitimate pluralism of expression."*

**Example:** Matthew writes for Jewish Christians (Jesus as new Moses); Luke writes for Gentiles (Jesus, Saviour of all). One Christ, two lenses — legitimate pluralism.

---

## B. THE PATRISTIC PERIOD (The Era of the Fathers of the Church)

**Specification / dates:** from the apostolic period to **mid-8th century**, marked by the deaths of:
- **John Damascene (749 AD)** in the East;
- **Gregory the Great (604 AD)** and **Isidore of Seville (639 AD)** in the West.

**Driving need:** to develop a **theological vocabulary** to *protect the Church's faith*.

### 1. The Apostolic Fathers
- **Clement of Rome, Ignatius (of Antioch), Polycarp.**
- Concerns were *"pastoral, didactic and exhortatory"* (pastoral care, teaching, encouragement) — not yet systematic.

### 2. The Apologists
- **Justin (Martyr), Irenaeus, Clement of Alexandria.**
- Wrote *in defense* of Christianity to win *"official recognition of the rights of Christians"* from the emperors.
- *Apologist = a defender of the faith (Greek apologia = defense).*

### 3. The Alexandrian School (Greek)
- Centred in **Alexandria**; engaged the Greek world.
- Figures: **Clement, Origen**, and the **Cappadocian Fathers — Gregory Nazianzen, Basil of Caesarea, Gregory of Nyssa.**
- **Christological tendency:** e.g., **Cyril of Alexandria** *"focused on the divinity of Jesus Christ and tended to downplay His humanity."*
- *Style: allegorical interpretation, emphasis on Christ's divinity.*

### 4. The Antiochene School
- In contrast to Alexandria. Figure: **Theodore of Mopsuestia.**
- Asserted the *"full humanity of Jesus Christ"* but *"tended to loosen His union with the divinity."*
- *Style: literal/historical interpretation, emphasis on Christ's humanity.*

**Specification — the two schools as a polarity:**

| School | Emphasis | Risk |
|--------|----------|------|
| Alexandrian | Christ's **divinity** | Downplaying His humanity |
| Antiochene | Christ's **humanity** | Loosening His unity with divinity |

*These tensions drove the great Christological councils (Nicaea, Ephesus, Chalcedon).*

### 5. The Latin Fathers
- Encountered **Latin culture** in Rome and Carthage.
- Pioneers: **Hippolytus, Tertullian**; consolidated by **Ambrose of Milan** and **Augustine of Hippo.**

---

## C. THE TRANSITION TO SCHOLASTICISM (The Aristotelian Turn)

**Specification:** early Christian reflection used *"Semitic categories of expression, which were basically functional in character"* (concerned with action, story, function).

**The great shift:** when **Aristotle's** works reached Western Europe, theologians had to *"formulate in a precise and technical manner the truth that Sacred Scripture had expressed in concrete symbolic terms."*

**Key phrase (memorize):**
> *"The focus of reflection was gradually shifted from the plane of action to the plane of being."*

*Simple meaning:* theology moved from asking "What does God **do**?" (Hebrew, functional) to "What is God's **being/nature**?" (Greek, metaphysical).

**The tension Aristotle created:**
- His *"naturalistic view of reality"* said the cosmos has an internal consistency reason can map — which seemed to clash with the Christian view of reality as *"completely and always dependent on God."*
- Result: a *"crying need for a 'second order' of reflection in order to clarify and defend the faith"* using the new philosophical tools.
- *"Second-order reflection" = stepping back to think **about** the faith systematically, using philosophy — theology as a science.*

---

## D. SCHOLASTICISM (c. 800–1500)

**Specification — four sub-periods:**
- **Pre-Scholasticism** (800–1050)
- **Early Scholasticism** (1050–1200)
- **High Scholasticism** (1200–1350)
- **Late Scholasticism** (1350–1500)

**Setting:** developed within the new **university system**, driven by *"lectures and disputations."*
- *Disputation = a formal public debate — pose a question, argue objections, resolve them. The engine of Scholastic method.*

### Key Figures (with dates and defining ideas)

**1. Anselm of Canterbury (1033–1109) — "Father of Scholasticism"**
- Theology = *"faith seeking understanding"* (*fides quaerens intellectum*), giving an *"insight into faith."*
- He held that showing the *"reasonableness and necessity"* of the truths of faith actually **defends** them — extending even to Trinity, Incarnation, Redemption.

**2. Thomas Aquinas (1225–1274) — Intellect / Theology as Sacred Science**
- Theology is a *"sacred science"* (*scientia*) that *"borrows its principles from the science of God made known to us by revelation."*
- Replaced *"the way of discovery (via inventionis)"* with *"the way of exposition (via doctrinae)."*
  - *Via inventionis = personally searching out the truth; via doctrinae = laying it out in orderly teaching once found.*

**3. Bonaventure (1221–1274) — Will / Mystical Theology**
- Theology is *"an itinerary of the soul toward God"* (a travel-route of the soul).
- Philosophy is only a helper: theologians use it *"as the children of Israel carried away the treasures of Egypt."*

**4. John Duns Scotus (1266–1308) — Christocentric Synthesis**
- Built on *"God is love,"* so everything originates in love.

**5. William of Ockham (1285–1347) — Nominalism / Breakdown of the Synthesis**
- **Ockham's Razor:** *"Entia non sunt multiplicanda sine necessitate"* — "beings/entities should not be multiplied without necessity" (prefer the simplest explanation).
- Rejected **universals** (*nominalism* = only individual things are real; "universals" are just names).
- Argued theology is *"not a science"* because its principles *rely on revelation rather than reason*.
- **Specification:** Ockham's nominalism dissolved the neat Scholastic synthesis of faith and reason, separating them.

---

## E. THE POST-SCHOLASTIC PERIOD (16th–19th centuries)

### 1. The Protestant Reformation
- **Reaction against** highly speculative theology and moral corruption in the medieval Church.
- **Martin Luther** and **John Calvin** championed *"Scripture Alone"* (*Sola Scriptura*).
- *Sola Scriptura = Scripture as the sole final authority, over against Tradition + Magisterium.*

### 2. The Council of Trent
- The Catholic Church **rallied to defend its doctrines** against the Reformers.
- *Trent (1545–1563) shaped Catholic theology for the next 400 years — on Scripture-and-Tradition, justification, sacraments.*

### 3. Neo-Scholasticism
- Triggered by **Pope Leo XIII's 1879 encyclical *Aeterni Patris***, which **revived the teachings of Thomas Aquinas.**
- *"Neo" = new; a modern revival of Thomistic (Aquinas-based) theology as the Church's standard framework.*

---

## F. MODERN AND CONTEMPORARY THEOLOGY (20th–21st centuries)

### 1. The Second Vatican Council (1962–1965)
- A **watershed** that *broke the dominance of the Neo-Scholastic paradigm* and shifted the Church toward a global *"World Church"* perspective.
- *Watershed = a decisive turning-point. "World Church" = no longer Euro-centred but genuinely global, valuing every culture's theology.*

### 2. Contextual Theologies
- **Core principle:** all theology is shaped by its environment — so modern theology focuses on **specific cultural and social realities.**

**Major contextual theologies (with defining marks):**

| Theology | Origin | Defining focus |
|----------|--------|----------------|
| **Theology of Secularisation** | D. Bonhoeffer | *"to genuinely Christianise the world is to secularise it"* |
| **Black Theology** | USA | The Word of God for the historical/social life of the **Black community** |
| **Feminist Theology / Ecofeminism** | Western | Gender discrimination; links *"the oppression of women and the rest of nature"* |
| **Dalit Theology** | India | Protests systems creating a *"false consciousness of pollution"* for the outcastes |
| **Minjung Theology** | Korea | The *"powerless people"* and their repressed anger (*han*); Jesus as ultimate victim of oppression |

**Term explainers:**
- *Ecofeminism = links the domination of women and the domination of nature as one connected injustice.*
- *Dalit "false consciousness of pollution" = the false, imposed belief that outcaste persons are inherently "polluting/impure"; Dalit theology exposes and rejects this using the liberating dimension of the Gospel.*
- *Minjung = Korean for "the mass of ordinary powerless people"; han = deep accumulated grief/resentment of the oppressed.*

**Specification — the pluralism principle vindicated:** these theologies show, in the modern age, exactly what the Bible already displayed — one Christ, many legitimate contextual expressions.
